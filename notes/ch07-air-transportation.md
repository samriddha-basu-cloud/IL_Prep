## 1. Introduction ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
**Air freight** refers to goods moved by loading them in an aircraft and sending them to their destination via aerial routes. It is popular because it **ensures passage for goods to anywhere in the world an aircraft can fly to and land**. E-commerce customers (Amazon, Alibaba, eBay) increasingly expect swift, expedited delivery, which rapid air freight allows.

### Types of air cargo services (by speed) 📘
- **Express air service** — fastest, time-definite (next-flight-out, door to door).
- **Standard air service** — normal scheduled service, a few days.
- **Deferred air service** — slower and cheaper; the forwarder holds cargo to consolidate and fly when space is cheap.

### Routing 📘
- **Direct routing** — gives the shipper **more supply chain flexibility**; the extra **speed to market** benefits both production and distribution.
- **Indirect routing** (via hubs/transfer points) — **most cost-effective** method in many cases and lets customers send freight to some of the **most remote areas** of the world.

### Institutions 📘
- **Airports Authority of India (AAI)** manages air cargo logistics in India; its **Department of Cargo** establishes, manages and develops cargo terminals at airports. 📗 (Many terminals are now run by AAI Cargo Logistics & Allied Services Co. — AAICLAS — and private operators such as Menzies, Celebi, WFS at PPP airports.)
- **International Air Transport Association (IATA)** — a **non-political, commercial regulatory organisation** with powers to regulate the acts of its members. Main objective: **safe, regular and economical air transport** for the benefit of people worldwide, including threshold tariff rates.
- 📗 **ICAO** (International Civil Aviation Organization, UN agency, Chicago Convention 1944) sets government standards; **DGCA** regulates civil aviation in India; **BCAS** handles aviation security; **CBIC/Customs** clears cargo (Air Cargo Complexes).

## 2. Terms in air logistics ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
- **Unit Load Devices (ULDs)** — "the shipping containers of the skies"; many are shaped to the **aircraft hold** and so are specific to certain aircraft. **IATA has a system for identifying ULDs** (📗 e.g. code *AKE 12345 AA*: type, serial number, owner — *AKE* = LD3 container). Types: **pallets** (with nets) and **containers** (LD3, LD7, etc.). ULD regulations matter because ULDs are a leading cause of ground damage to aircraft.
- **Air Waybill (AWB)** — a **contract to transport goods by air**, issued by the carrier airline; it **limits the airline's liability** and details the goods carried. 📗 An AWB is **not a document of title** (non-negotiable), unlike an ocean B/L; it has an 11-digit number (3-digit airline prefix + 8 digits), 3 originals and several copies, and serves as receipt, contract evidence, freight bill, customs declaration support and insurance certificate (if insured).
- **House Air Waybill (HAWB)** — issued by a **freight forwarding company entitled to do so** (e.g. an **IATA cargo agent**, which has the relevant insurances and, as issuer, assumes liability like an airline does under an AWB). Used mostly when **small shipments are consolidated** by the forwarder for onward shipment.
- **Master Air Waybill (MAWB)** — the contract between the **freight forwarder and the carrier airline**; it details all the HAWBs consolidated in the shipment.
- **Other documentation** — packing lists, commercial invoices, certificates of origin and other cargo- or country-specific documents may be required at origin and destination airports, by airlines, security services and customs.
- **Air hubs and spokes** — because air transport has **high unit costs** (relatively low cargo volumes compared with other modes, long distances and high aircraft operating costs), carriers use **hubs** located strategically around the world near air cargo markets, employed on **major trade routes** (📗 e.g. FedEx at Memphis, UPS at Louisville, DHL at Leipzig/Cincinnati, Emirates at Dubai).

## 3. Advantages and disadvantages of air freight ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Speed** — better customer service, increased production | **Risk** — human error, extreme weather, turbulence, cargo fires, hazardous materials, regulatory compliance issues, strikes, pandemics, natural disasters, political upheaval, conflicts, economic downturns |
| **Reliability** — security against theft, less damage | **High operational costs** |
| **Accuracy** — fast track & trace, loss prevention | **Limited capacity** — aircraft size, number of cargo planes, flight schedules, peak-season surges, geopolitical issues |
| **Control** — reduced storage costs, manage perishability | **Dependent on weather** |
| **Scheduling** — direct shipping and indirect shipping options | **Unfit for specific products** |

### Products unfit for (or restricted in) air transport 📘
Flammable liquids (gasoline, alcohol, acetone, some paints); flammable solids (matches, lighters, some powders); flammable gases (propane, butane, oxygen); explosives (fireworks, flares, dynamite, ammunition); aerosol cans; cigarette lighters and refills; pesticides, chemicals and infectious materials; radioactive materials (uranium, plutonium); corrosives (acids, mercury); **lithium batteries**; magnetised materials; perishables needing temperature control; dry ice, specimens or samples; detergents, bleaches, drain/oven cleaners; machinery with internal combustion engines. 📗 Many of these can fly as **dangerous goods** if classified, packed, marked, labelled and documented per the **IATA DGR** (9 hazard classes) with a Shipper's Declaration; some are forbidden entirely.

### What moves by air 📘
- **General cargo** — clothes, books, electronics, leather products, gems and jewellery: non-perishable, non-hazardous, minimal handling → easy and cost-effective by air.
- **Urgent/high-value** — radiopharmaceuticals, high-value technology, scientific equipment, high-value commodities.
- **Perishable food** — fresh fish, meat, vegetables, exotic fruits, cut flowers.

## 4. Characteristics of air transport ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
1. **Fastest and most expensive** mode — used for expensive/important cargo and passengers.
2. Manufacture and operation of aircraft need detailed arrangements: **hangars, landing, fuelling, maintenance**.
3. Connects places at **long distances**, spreading economic activity to them.
4. Aircraft fly to **pre-decided schedules**.
5. Aircraft fly through **specified air corridors**.
6. **Not suitable for heavy goods and machinery** for commercial purposes.
7. Suitable for **relief operations** in calamities and disasters.
8. Air freight is usually **4–5 times the cost of road** transport and about **12–16 times the cost of sea** transport.
9. Can help create **competitive advantage**.
10. Can be used for **short shelf-life products** in a cost-effective manner.
11. **Customs clearance** needs smooth integration of customs processes, inspection, cargo handling and warehousing so cargo moves out of the airport easily.

## 5. Structure of the industry ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
- **IATA and trade associations** — set standards of operation (safety, security, training, ULDs and many more) and provide services such as **CASS (Cargo Accounts Settlement System)**: a web-based system that lets airlines and freight forwarders **make only one payment a month** to, or receive one payment from, airlines/cargo agents — greatly reducing paperwork.
- **Airlines** — own or lease and operate the aircraft carrying passengers and cargo (📗 combination carriers vs all-cargo carriers vs integrators like FedEx, UPS, DHL).
- **Lessors** — own aircraft and lease them to airlines when owning is unfavourable for the airline.
- **Cargo agents** — freight forwarders **licensed by IATA** to handle freight for customers; IATA sets standards, ensures they are insured and allows them to issue **HAWBs**.
- **Airport authorities** — own or lease and operate the airport infrastructure.
- 📗 Also: ground handlers/cargo terminal operators, customs brokers, GSAs (general sales agents), trucking (road feeder services).

## 6. Factors affecting the price of air freight ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
1. **Density of the product** — weight per unit volume; **low-density products are priced by volume, high-density by weight**; density affects available loading space.
2. **Quantity of cargo** — higher volume → higher total transport cost (though lower per-kg rate at weight breaks).
3. **Distance of travel** — drives variable costs (maintenance, fuel).
4. **Shape of the product** — affects load handling; odd shapes need special care, machinery and vehicles.
5. **Type of product** — determines storage and transport conditions; perishables need refrigerated freighters; pharma needs temperature and humidity control.
Plus (pricing chapter): route supply and demand, forwarder buying power, currency, fuel and war-risk surcharges, security screening and transit-time level.

## 7. IE Code (Importer-Exporter Code) ⭐⭐ 📘📗
The PPT's IE CODES slides: in India, **no person or firm can import or export without an IEC** (except exempted categories). It is issued by the **Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT)** under the Foreign Trade (Development & Regulation) Act 1992; it is a **10-digit code, now the same as the firm's PAN**; applied for online on the DGFT portal (fee ₹500); **lifetime validity**, but must be **updated annually (April–June)** or it is deactivated. It is quoted on shipping bills, bills of entry, AWBs and for bank remittances, and is needed for export benefits (RoDTEP, drawback). Exempt: personal-use imports, certain government bodies and charitable organisations.

## 8. Types of air services ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
1. **Air mail services** — the only option for shipping letters internationally.
2. **Express airfreight services** — cargo with a **guaranteed predetermined delivery date** (DHL, FedEx, UPS).
3. **Scheduled airfreight services** — offered on a **routine basis**; highly reliable and efficient, resulting in **relatively low-cost** delivery.
4. **Charter airfreight services** — cargo that can only go on a **charter aircraft (private terminal)** because it is **too heavy, too bulky, or its destination is not served by scheduled service**.
5. **Leased cargo aircraft services** — **"dry" lease** (aircraft only), **"damp" lease** (aircraft + some crew), **"wet" lease** or **ACMI lease** (Aircraft, Crew, Maintenance and Insurance provided by the lessor; lessee pays fuel, landing and handling).
6. **Airfreight forwarder services** — forwarders contract with air carriers, **consolidate shipments, buy space on flights**, and arrange **intermodal surface transport**; some provide full supply chain management services.

## 9. Types of aircraft ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
- **Passenger aircraft** — spare (belly) capacity used for expensive or important cargo, sometimes carried by an on-board courier. (📗 Belly cargo carries about half of world air cargo.)
- **Quick-change (QC) aircraft** — quickly converted from all-cargo to all-passenger service using **palletised seat sections** (passengers by day, cargo by night).
- **Combination (combi) aircraft** — carries **both cargo and passengers on the main deck** at the same time.
- **Air freighters (cargo aircraft)** — dedicated to carrying cargo (📗 e.g. Boeing 747-8F, 777F, A330P2F; nose-door freighters for outsized cargo; Antonov An-124/An-225 for heavy charters).

## 10. How air freight works — the 15 steps ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
(Infographic by International Cargo Express.)
1. **Source a supplier** that can comply with air transport.
2. **Decide on Incoterms** (international commercial terms) — typically FCA, CPT or CIP.
3. **Engage a freight forwarder.**
4. **Provide commercial documents** (invoice, packing list, certificates).
5. **Define the volumetric ratio** of your cargo (to calculate the cost of the air freight — chargeable weight).
6. **Pack your cargo** complying with all **air freight security regulations**.
7. **Review your route and airline options** with your freight forwarder.
8. **Provide the documentation** to your forwarder to arrange your shipment.
9. Once your flight is booked, an **air waybill** is produced and provided to you once your goods have departed.
10. **Goods in transit.**
11. The forwarder **commences customs clearance** for your cargo **prior to its arrival** (pre-arrival filing).
12. **Cargo arrives** at destination.
13. The **cargo terminal operator retrieves the cargo** from the aircraft and **stores it under customs bond** awaiting clearance and collection.
14. The **transport operator collects** the cargo (you may need a tail-lift truck).
15. The cargo is **delivered to its final destination**.

## 12. IATA — vision, mission, manuals ⭐⭐ 📘
- **IATA** is the trade association for the world's airlines; it supports many areas of aviation activity and helps formulate industry policy on critical aviation issues. Founded 1945 (Havana), HQ Montreal.
- **Vision**: working together to shape the future growth of a safe, secure and sustainable air transport industry that connects and enriches our world. **Mission**: to represent, lead and serve the airline industry.
- **Numbers (PPT)**: **290 member airlines**, **400 strategic partners**, **100,000 IATA-accredited agents**, plus standards, manuals and guidelines.
- **Services by area**: airlines, airports, air traffic management, freight forwarders, safety, governments & policy makers, ground handlers, industry partners, travel agents; priorities include environment and sustainability, diversity, commercial, effective operations.
- **What are IATA manuals?** The regulations needed for preparing, handling or accepting different types of goods — live animals, airport handling, air cargo tariffs and rules.
- **How are they developed?** From recommendations of governing bodies such as **ICAO** and standards built by member-airline delegates and industry associations collaborating with IATA.
- **Why updated every year?** Over **350 changes** are made each year to keep manuals compliant with the newest regulations and trends.
- **Why use IATA manuals?** Neutral organisation; 75+ years of experience; standardised guidelines; for and by the industry; updated regularly.
- **Five key benefits** (users surveyed): **experience reduction in errors 87%**, **avoid fines on air cargo shipments 81%**, **avoid customs delays from incorrect labelling or documentation 79%**, **reduced risk of aircraft damage 79%**, **speed up shipment processing 70%**.

### IATA manuals and regulations (knowledge only) 📘
| Area | Manual | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Dangerous goods | **DGR** — Dangerous Goods Regulations | Safe, efficient carriage of dangerous goods; used daily by airlines, forwarders, ground handlers, shippers |
| | **LBSR** — Lithium Battery Shipping Regulations | Packaging, labelling, marking of lithium batteries; failure can cause fire |
| | **ISSR** — Infectious Substance Shipping Regulations | Plain-language guide to shipping infectious substances (lab samples) |
| Special cargo | **PCR** — Perishable Cargo Regulations | Best practice for perishables; cold-chain failures often from poor documents, labels, packaging |
| | **TCR** — Temperature Control Regulations | Shipping temperature-sensitive (healthcare) products |
| | **LAR** — Live Animals Regulations | Animals arrive in good health |
| | Compassionate Transportation manual | Human remains — funeral operators, airlines, forwarders |
| Cargo operations | **ICHM** — IATA Cargo Handling Manual | No. 1 source for cargo operations standards |
| | **ULDR** — ULD Regulations | ULDs are the leading cause of ground damage; safe ULD handling |
| | **PLACI** — Pre-loading Advance Cargo Information | Harmonised advance cargo data filing (US, then EU, UK, Canada) |
| Ground operations | **AHM** — Airport Handling Manual | Industry policies/standards above and below the wing |
| | **IGOM** — IATA Ground Operations Manual | Standardised ground and ramp procedures |
| | **BRM** — Baggage Reference Manual | Best practice to reduce mishandled bags |

## Self-test
**Q1. Differentiate AWB, MAWB and HAWB.**
AWB: the airline's contract of carriage and receipt, limiting its liability; non-negotiable. MAWB: the AWB issued by the airline to the forwarder for a consolidated shipment, listing the HAWBs. HAWB: issued by an IATA forwarder to each individual shipper in a consolidation; the forwarder assumes carrier-like liability.

**Q2. What is a ULD?**
A unit load device — a pallet or container shaped to the aircraft hold, identified by IATA's coding system, used to load cargo efficiently and safely.

**Q3. Explain the hub-and-spoke system in air cargo.**
Because air cargo has high unit costs (low volumes, long distances, high aircraft costs), carriers feed cargo from many origins (spokes) into strategic hubs and consolidate it onto larger flights on major trade routes, raising load factors and reach.

**Q4. List five advantages and five disadvantages of air freight.**
Advantages: speed, reliability/security, accuracy (track and trace), control (less storage, perishables), scheduling flexibility. Disadvantages: risk, high operating cost, limited capacity, weather dependence, unfit for some products.

**Q5. Name the six types of air services.**
Air mail, express airfreight, scheduled airfreight, charter airfreight, leased cargo aircraft (dry, damp, wet/ACMI), airfreight forwarder services.

**Q6. What is CASS?**
IATA's Cargo Accounts Settlement System: a web-based billing and settlement system that lets airlines and agents make or receive one payment a month instead of many.

**Q7. What factors affect air freight price?**
Density, quantity, distance, shape and type of product (plus route demand, fuel and security surcharges, transit time).

**Q8. How much more expensive is air than road and sea?**
About 4–5 times road and 12–16 times sea freight.

**Q9. What is an IEC?**
The 10-digit Importer-Exporter Code (same as PAN) issued by DGFT, mandatory for importing or exporting from India, valid for life but updated annually.
