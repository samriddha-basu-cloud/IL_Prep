# Glossary — International Logistics Terms A–Z
Exam-ready one- or two-line definitions. 📘 = term from your course PPTs, PDFs or problem files; 📗 = standard textbook term added for completeness. Use the flashcards and quiz views to self-test.

## 0–9
- **1PL to 5PL** 📗: Levels of logistics outsourcing: shipper's own logistics (1PL), asset-based carrier (2PL), contract logistics provider (3PL), integrator managing 3PLs (4PL), network aggregator (5PL).
- **2M alliance** 📘: Vessel-sharing alliance between Maersk and MSC formed in 2015 that shaped freight rates through shared capacity.
- **4 Ts of risk** 📗: Responses to a risk: treat (mitigate), transfer, tolerate, terminate (avoid).
- **7 steps of shipping cost** 📘: Export transport, export clearance, origin handling, freight, import clearance, destination handling, import transport.

## A
- **ACC3** 📘: EU designation for air carriers flying cargo into the EU from third-country airports; since 2014 their origin screening must be validated and approved.
- **ACMI lease** 📘: "Wet" lease in which the lessor provides Aircraft, Crew, Maintenance and Insurance.
- **Actual total loss** 📗: Insured goods destroyed or irretrievably lost.
- **Air waybill (AWB)** 📘: Contract of carriage by air issued by the airline; details the goods and limits the airline's liability; not a document of title.
- **Airports Authority of India (AAI)** 📘: Manages air cargo logistics in India; its Department of Cargo develops cargo terminals.
- **All-in rate** 📘: Freight rate that includes all transport costs, possibly door-to-door, in one figure.
- **Audit rights** 📗: Contract clause letting the buyer inspect the supplier's premises, records and compliance.
- **Average adjuster** 📗: Independent expert who determines GA items, collects security and prepares the GA adjustment.

## B
- **BAF (Bunker Adjustment Factor)** 📘: Ocean surcharge for fluctuating fuel costs, updated monthly or quarterly.
- **BATNA** 📘: Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement — a party's fallback if negotiation fails.
- **Bill of lading (B/L)** 📗: Ocean carrier's receipt, evidence of contract of carriage and document of title.
- **Billing accuracy** 📘: Accurate freight bills ÷ total freight bills; typical target > 99%.
- **Bulk container** 📘: Container with top hatches for free-flowing dry cargo such as cement, ores and grain.

## C
- **CAF (Currency Adjustment Factor)** 📘: Surcharge offsetting exchange-rate fluctuations, often a % of base freight plus BAF.
- **CASS** 📘: IATA's Cargo Accounts Settlement System letting airlines and agents make or receive one payment a month.
- **CAx (Container Availability Index)** 📘: Container xChange index of container availability; 0.5 = balance, above = surplus, below = shortage.
- **CFR (Cost and Freight)** 📘: Sea Incoterm; seller pays freight to destination port; risk passes on board at port of shipment.
- **Chargeable weight** 📘: The greater of actual and volumetric weight, used to price freight.
- **Charter airfreight** 📘: Cargo shipped on a chartered aircraft because it is too heavy, too bulky or the destination isn't served.
- **CIC (Container Imbalance Charge)** 📘: Surcharge compensating carriers for repositioning empty containers on imbalanced lanes.
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight)** 📘: Sea Incoterm; seller pays freight and minimum insurance (ICC C, 110%) to destination port; risk passes on board.
- **CIP (Carriage and Insurance Paid To)** 📘: Any-mode Incoterm; seller pays carriage and all-risks insurance (ICC A, 110%); risk passes at first carrier.
- **Combi aircraft** 📘: Aircraft carrying cargo and passengers on the main deck at the same time.
- **Connectainer** 📘: 20-ft container that can be joined with another to form a 40-ft unit.
- **Constructive total loss** 📗: Loss where saving or repairing the goods would cost more than their value; notice of abandonment is given.
- **Container** 📘: Sealed, rigid, reusable box for carrying goods without reloading.
- **Container sharing** 📘: Carriers or shippers pooling containers to reduce empty moves.
- **Container substitution** 📘: Meeting demand with another container type, e.g. one 40' for two 20' requests.
- **Containerization** 📘: Transporting cargo in ISO-standardised containers that move across modes without handling the goods.
- **Contract rate** 📘: Freight rate agreed for a set volume over a period between shipper and line.
- **Contributory value** 📘: Value of each saved interest (ship, cargo, freight) on which GA contributions are calculated.
- **CPT (Carriage Paid To)** 📘: Any-mode Incoterm; seller pays carriage to named destination; risk passes at first carrier; no insurance obligation.

## D
- **DAP (Delivered at Place)** 📘: Seller delivers to the named place ready for unloading; buyer clears import and pays duty.
- **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)** 📘: Seller delivers to the named place and clears import, paying duties — maximum seller obligation.
- **Deferred air service** 📘: Slower, cheaper air service where cargo waits for consolidation.
- **Demurrage** 📗: Charge for a container staying inside the port/terminal beyond free time.
- **Detention** 📗: Charge for keeping a carrier's container outside the terminal beyond free time.
- **DIM factor** 📘: Divisor converting volume to dimensional weight: 6,000 cm³/kg air; 139 (domestic) or 166 (international) in³/lb.
- **Dimensional weight** 📘: Weight equivalent of a shipment's volume; L × W × H ÷ DIM factor.
- **DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded)** 📘: Seller delivers and unloads at the named place — the only rule with seller unloading; replaced DAT.
- **Dry container** 📘: The most common general-purpose container for dry cargo like coal, grain and metals.

## E
- **EBS (Emergency Bunker Surcharge)** 📘: Fuel surcharge used in crises such as the Red Sea disruption.
- **Empty container repositioning (ECR)** 📘: Moving empty containers from surplus to deficit locations without cargo.
- **EXW (Ex Works)** 📘: Seller makes goods available at its premises; buyer loads, clears export and bears all costs and risks — minimum seller obligation.
- **Express airfreight** 📘: Air cargo with a guaranteed predetermined delivery date (DHL, FedEx, UPS).

## F
- **FAS (Free Alongside Ship)** 📘: Sea Incoterm; seller places goods alongside the buyer's vessel at the port of shipment.
- **FCA (Free Carrier)** 📘: Any-mode Incoterm; seller delivers to the buyer's carrier at a named place and clears export; preferred for containers.
- **FCL (Full Container Load)** 📘: A whole container booked by one shipper; better above about 15 m³.
- **FEU** 📗: Forty-foot equivalent unit = 2 TEU.
- **Fishbone diagram** 📘: Ishikawa cause-and-effect diagram grouping root causes (6Ms) of a problem.
- **Flat rack** 📘: Container with a strong base and collapsible ends for odd-size cargo.
- **Flat rate** 📘: One fixed freight charge regardless of distance or weight.
- **FOB (Free On Board)** 📘: Sea Incoterm; seller loads goods on the buyer's nominated vessel; risk passes on board.
- **FOB Destination** 📘: US freight term where the seller owns goods in transit and files claims until delivery.
- **FOB Origin** 📘: US freight term where the buyer owns goods in transit from the shipping point and files claims.
- **Foldable container** 📘: Collapsible container (e.g. four folded inside a fifth) that saves up to 75% of space when empty.
- **Force majeure** 📘: Clause excusing performance when an event beyond a party's control (war, disaster, lockdown) prevents it.
- **Freight Collect and Allowed** 📘: Buyer pays the carrier then deducts the freight from its payment, so the seller bears the cost.
- **Freight forwarder** 📘: Company that organises international shipments across modes, documents and customs on the shipper's behalf.
- **Freight Prepaid and Charged Back** 📘: Seller pays the carrier then adds freight to the invoice, so the buyer bears the cost.
- **Freighter** 📘: Aircraft dedicated to carrying cargo.
- **FTL (Full Truckload)** 📘: Whole truck bought by one shipper — about 33 pallets or 90 m³.

## G
- **General Average (GA)** 📘: Intentional, reasonable extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure for the common safety of ship and cargo, shared rateably by all saved interests.
- **GA bond** 📗: Undertaking by the cargo receiver to pay its share of GA, signed before cargo release.
- **GA rate** 📘: Total GA loss ÷ total contributory value × 100.
- **Garment container** 📘: Container with rails and hangers so clothes travel hanging, without creasing.
- **GRI (General Rate Increase)** 📘: Temporary carrier-announced price rise on volatile trade lanes.

## H
- **H&M insurance** 📘: Hull and Machinery insurance covering the ship and its share of GA.
- **HAWB (House Air Waybill)** 📘: Air waybill issued by a freight forwarder/IATA agent to each shipper in a consolidation.
- **Hidden costs** 📘: Costs of outsourcing beyond the contract price — audits, travel, integration, inspection, contract management.
- **Hubs and spokes** 📘: Air network that feeds cargo into strategically located hubs to offset high unit costs.

## I
- **IATA** 📘: International Air Transport Association — non-political commercial trade body of airlines (290 members) setting standards and services.
- **IATA cargo agent** 📘: Freight forwarder licensed by IATA to handle air cargo and issue HAWBs.
- **ICC (Institute Cargo Clauses)** 📗: Standard marine cargo covers: A (all risks), B and C (named perils).
- **IE Code (IEC)** 📘: 10-digit Importer-Exporter Code (same as PAN) issued by DGFT, mandatory to import or export from India.
- **Incoterms®** 📘: ICC's 11 standard trade terms (2020) defining seller and buyer tasks, costs and risk transfer.
- **Insulated container** 📘: Thermal container using dry ice for fruit and vegetables.
- **Insurable interest** 📗: Financial stake in the goods required to claim on insurance.
- **Intermodal transportation** 📘: Moving goods in one load unit (container, trailer, swap body) by several modes without handling the goods.
- **ISO** 📘: International Organization for Standardization, which standardised container sizes, ratings and terms.
- **ISPS charge** 📘: Ship and port facility security surcharge.

## K
- **Known cargo / known shipper** 📘: Cargo screened and kept secure (or from a validated shipper) that may be loaded without re-screening.
- **KPI** 📘: Key performance indicator — a measurable value showing how well a process meets its objectives.

## L
- **Landed cost** 📘: Purchase price plus all costs to get the goods to the buyer (freight, duty, insurance, handling, inventory cost).
- **LCL (Less than Container Load)** 📘: Shared container for small shipments (under ~15 m³), priced per m³.
- **Leased cargo aircraft** 📘: Aircraft obtained on dry, damp, wet or ACMI lease.
- **Liquidated damages** 📘: Pre-agreed sum payable for a specified breach, such as delay, without proving actual loss.
- **LTL (Less than Truckload)** 📘: Shared truck service for 1–2 pallets with hub handling.

## M
- **MAWB (Master Air Waybill)** 📘: Contract between forwarder and airline covering a consolidated shipment of many HAWBs.
- **Montreal Convention** 📘: 1999 air carriage treaty limiting cargo liability (22 SDR/kg per the course text).
- **Multi-sourcing** 📘: Buying an item from several suppliers to reduce disruption risk.

## N
- **Nearshoring** 📘: Moving production or services to a nearby country (US → Mexico, EU → Poland).
- **NMFC freight class** 📘: US LTL classification (50–500) based on density, stowability, handling and liability.
- **NVOCC** 📘: Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier — issues its own B/L and resells space bought from shipping lines.
- **NVO** 📘: Non-vessel operator — intermediary between shippers and lines that doesn't own ships.

## O
- **Offshoring** 📘: Moving an activity to another country, whether in-house or outsourced.
- **On-time delivery** 📘: On-time deliveries ÷ total deliveries; typical target > 95%.
- **Open top container** 📘: Container without a fixed roof for cargo taller than the box.
- **Outsourcing** 📘: Contracting a third party to perform an activity previously or potentially done in-house.

## P
- **P&I club** 📗: Mutual insurer of shipowners' third-party liabilities.
- **Particular Average (PA)** 📘: Accidental partial loss borne only by the owner of the damaged property.
- **Payback period** 📘: One-time investment ÷ annual savings.
- **Perfect delivery index** 📘: On-time % × damage-free % × billing accuracy %; target > 95%.
- **PSS (Peak Season Surcharge)** 📘: Surcharge in high-demand seasons such as August–October.

## Q
- **Quick-change aircraft** 📘: Aircraft rapidly converted between all-cargo and all-passenger use with palletised seats.

## R
- **Reefer** 📘: Refrigerated container with its own cooling unit for meat, fish and other perishables.
- **Risk matrix** 📘: Grid scoring risks by probability × impact to prioritise mitigation.

## S
- **Salvage** 📗: Reward to a voluntary salvor for saving maritime property; allowed in GA when for common safety.
- **Scheduled airfreight** 📘: Routine air cargo services — reliable, efficient, relatively low cost.
- **Shipper** 📘: Owner of goods who arranges their transport and is responsible for packaging and labelling.
- **Single sourcing** 📘: Buying an item from one supplier — lowest cost but highest disruption risk.
- **SLA (Service Level Agreement)** 📘: Contract section defining KPIs, targets, measurement, penalties and incentives.
- **SOC (Shipper Owned Container)** 📘: Container owned or leased by the shipper, used to avoid CIC and carrier detention.
- **Spot rate** 📘: One-time freight price for a specific shipment at current market conditions.
- **Street turn** 📘: Reusing an import container directly for a nearby export load without returning it to port.
- **Subrogation** 📗: Insurer's right, after paying a claim, to recover from the party responsible.

## T
- **Tank container** 📘: Stainless-steel container for liquids such as milk or chemicals.
- **TEU** 📗: Twenty-foot equivalent unit, the standard unit of container capacity.
- **THC (Terminal Handling Charge)** 📘: Charge for container handling at origin or destination terminals.
- **Transit time average** 📘: Sum of transit times ÷ total deliveries; aim for low variation.
- **Tunnel container** 📘: Container that opens at both ends for fast loading.

## U
- **ULD (Unit Load Device)** 📘: Pallet or container shaped to an aircraft hold — "the shipping containers of the skies".

## V
- **Ventilated container** 📘: Thermal container with side openings for air circulation, for tea and coffee.
- **Volumetric weight** 📘: Volume converted to weight (air: m³ × 166.67 kg; ocean: 1 m³ = 1,000 kg).

## W
- **Weighted scoring model** 📘: Supplier or carrier selection by Σ weight × rating across criteria.
- **W/M (weight or measure)** 📗: Ocean pricing on the greater of tonnes or cubic metres (revenue ton).

## Y
- **York-Antwerp Rules** 📗: International rules for adjusting General Average (latest 2016), incorporated in bills of lading.
