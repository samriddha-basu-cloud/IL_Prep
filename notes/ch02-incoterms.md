## 1. Why Incoterms are needed ⭐⭐⭐
The terms of trade in the contract of sale decide **which tasks are the exporter's and which are the importer's** 📘. An international shipment involves many tasks (packing, loading, export clearance, main carriage, insurance, import clearance, duties, delivery); dividing them afresh for every deal would be a daunting task, it is impossible to foresee everything that could go wrong in transit, and the result would be **misunderstandings, disputes and litigation**. Incoterms settle, in three letters, the **tasks (responsibilities), costs and risks** borne by buyers and sellers 📘.

Incoterms answer five questions 📘:
1. Which **tasks** the exporter performs.
2. Which tasks the importer performs.
3. Which activities are **paid** by the exporter.
4. Which activities are paid by the importer.
5. **When the transfer of responsibility (risk)** for the goods takes place.

### Purpose and importance 📘
- **Purpose**: transmission of **risks** in transporting the goods; allocation of **logistics costs** between seller and buyer; the **documents and customs formalities** for export and import.
- **Importance**: widely used and internationally known; they prevent confusion by clarifying obligations. Knowing them: (1) brings **clarity and certainty**, (2) **reduces costs** (fewer delays), (3) **reduces risks** (both sides know who bears what at each stage), (4) **simplifies communication**.

### History ⭐⭐
Published by the **International Chamber of Commerce (ICC)**, Paris, in **1936**; revised in **1953, 1967, 1976, 1980, 1990, 2000, 2010 and 2020** 📘 (the current version is **Incoterms® 2020**, in force from 1 January 2020). The ICC's mission: "to make business work for everyone, every day, everywhere" by promoting open international trade 📘. Incoterms are used in all the documents of an international transaction (contract, invoice, L/C, insurance, B/L).

### Transfer of risk vs transfer of title ⭐⭐⭐
- **Transfer of responsibility (risk)** is dictated by the Incoterms rule and coincides with **delivery**, a point clearly stated in each rule 📘.
- **Transfer of title (ownership)** is **not** covered by Incoterms; it usually passes when the importer has paid, obtained the original bill of lading, accepted a draft or done whatever the contract of sale says 📘.

### What Incoterms do NOT deal with ⭐⭐⭐
Whether there is a contract of sale at all; the specification of the goods; the time, place, method or currency of **payment** of the price; remedies for breach; most consequences of delay and other breaches; the effect of sanctions; the imposition of tariffs; export/import prohibitions; **force majeure or hardship**; intellectual property rights; and the method, venue or law of dispute resolution 📘. They also do **not** decide transfer of title or the price 📘.

### How to write an Incoterm correctly ⭐⭐
**[Rule] [named place or port] Incoterms® 2020** — e.g. *FCA Warehouse 3, Shanghai, Incoterms® 2020* 📘. Never just "FOB". Be precise about the place (a terminal, not just a city) because risk passes there.

## 2. The 11 rules and their classification ⭐⭐⭐
| Group (by letter) | Any mode or modes of transport (7) | Sea and inland waterway only (4) |
|---|---|---|
| **E — departure** | **EXW** Ex Works | — |
| **F — main carriage unpaid** (buyer pays main freight) | **FCA** Free Carrier | **FAS** Free Alongside Ship, **FOB** Free On Board |
| **C — main carriage paid** (seller pays main freight; risk passes at origin) | **CPT** Carriage Paid To, **CIP** Carriage and Insurance Paid To | **CFR** Cost and Freight, **CIF** Cost, Insurance and Freight |
| **D — arrival** (seller bears risk to destination) | **DAP** Delivered at Place, **DPU** Delivered at Place Unloaded, **DDP** Delivered Duty Paid | — |

- **Freight collect** terms (buyer pays main carriage): EXW, FCA, FAS, FOB. **Freight prepaid** terms: CFR, CIF, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP 📘 (IncoDocs chart).
- Air freight usually uses **FCA, CPT, CIP** — risk passes at the airport cargo terminal 📘.
- Maritime-only **FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF** suit **bulk or non-containerised** cargo where risk passes at the port of loading/on board the vessel 📘.
- The 7 rules suitable for road (and multimodal, containerised or air) are EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP 📘.

**Memory aid**: the seller's obligation grows **E → F → C → D**: EXW (minimum for seller) … DDP (maximum for seller).

## 3. Each rule in detail ⭐⭐⭐
The course slides rate each rule's risk, insurance and freight cost 📘; the table adds delivery point and customs.

| Rule | Delivery / risk transfer point | Main carriage paid by | Insurance | Export clearance | Import clearance & duty | Course rating 📘 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EXW** | Goods made available at **seller's premises**, not loaded | Buyer | Not required (buyer's interest) | **Buyer** | Buyer | Seller risk lowest, buyer highest; buyer bears most cost |
| **FCA** | Handed to **buyer's carrier** at named place (seller's premises → seller loads; elsewhere → ready for unloading) | Buyer | Not required | **Seller** | Buyer | Seller risk low; buyer high; buyer bears most cost |
| **FAS** | **Alongside the ship** at named port of shipment | Buyer | Not required | Seller | Buyer | Seller risk low; buyer high; buyer bears most cost |
| **FOB** | **On board the vessel** nominated by buyer at port of shipment | Buyer | Not required | Seller | Buyer | Seller medium, buyer medium; buyer bears most cost |
| **CFR** | **On board the vessel** at port of shipment (risk) — seller pays freight to destination port | Seller | Not required | Seller | Buyer | Seller medium, buyer medium; seller bears most cost |
| **CIF** | **On board** at port of shipment (risk) — seller pays freight + insurance to destination port | Seller | **Seller buys minimum cover: ICC (C), 110%** | Seller | Buyer | Insurance yes, only for the main (sea) carriage |
| **CPT** | Handed to the **first carrier** (risk) — seller pays carriage to named destination | Seller | Not required | Seller | Buyer | Seller low, buyer high; seller bears most cost |
| **CIP** | Handed to the **first carrier** (risk) — seller pays carriage + insurance to destination | Seller | **Seller buys all-risks cover: ICC (A), 110%** | Seller | Buyer | Insurance yes, only during transit |
| **DAP** | At **named destination place**, on arriving means of transport, **ready for unloading** | Seller | Not required (seller's own interest) | Seller | **Buyer** | Seller risk high, buyer low |
| **DPU** | At named destination, **unloaded** from the means of transport | Seller | Not required | Seller | Buyer | Seller risk highest, buyer lowest; **only rule where seller unloads** |
| **DDP** | At named destination, ready for unloading, **import-cleared and duty paid** | Seller | Not required | Seller | **Seller** | Maximum responsibility on seller |

### Key points per rule 📘📗
- **EXW** — seller's only obligation is to make goods available at its premises, suitably packed. Buyer loads, clears export, carries, insures, clears import. **Problem**: the buyer, a foreigner, often cannot legally file the export declaration in the seller's country (in India the exporter's IEC and shipping bill are needed), and the seller does not get proof of export for GST/duty refunds → **use FCA instead**.
- **FCA** — sits between seller-friendly EXW and buyer-friendly DAP/DDP 📘. Best for **containerised, air and road** freight 📘. Seller clears export. 2020 change: parties can agree the buyer's carrier issues an **on-board bill of lading** to the seller (needed for letters of credit).
- **FAS** — seller places goods alongside the buyer's vessel (on the quay or a barge); used for bulk, heavy-lift and break-bulk cargo.
- **FOB** — seller loads goods on the buyer's nominated vessel; from then all costs and risks are the buyer's 📘. Standard for **bulk commodities** 📘 (coal, grain, ore, coffee in bulk). **Not suitable for containers** — the seller loses control at the container yard days before loading, yet still bears risk until "on board". Use FCA.
- **CFR / CIF** — **two critical points**: risk passes at the port of **shipment** (on board), but costs pass at the port of **destination**. So in CFR/CIF, risk transfers **before** costs 📘. CIF insurance is minimum cover (**Institute Cargo Clauses C**) 📘 — buyer may ask for more.
- **CPT / CIP** — the multimodal versions of CFR / CIF: risk passes when goods are handed to the **first carrier**; seller pays to the named destination. **CIP** requires higher cover (**ICC A, all risks**) 📘 — best for **high-value goods** 📘.
- **DAP** — popular for sellers who want to offer flexibility while managing costs and risks 📘; seller delivers to buyer's named place, buyer unloads, clears import and pays duty. Replaced DAF, DES, DDU (2010).
- **DPU** — formerly **DAT** (Delivered at Terminal) in 2010; renamed in 2020 because the place need not be a terminal 📘. The only rule requiring the seller to **unload** 📘 — good for heavy/oversized cargo when the seller controls unloading equipment.
- **DDP** — seller does everything, including import clearance and duties/taxes (VAT/GST) 📘. **Avoid DDP if the seller is not experienced with the destination country's customs** 📘 or cannot register for import VAT there.

## 4. Main changes in Incoterms® 2020 (vs 2010) ⭐⭐
1. **DAT renamed DPU** (Delivered at Place Unloaded) — the place of unloading can be any place, not only a terminal.
2. **Different insurance levels**: CIF stays at ICC (C) minimum; **CIP raised to ICC (A)** all risks.
3. **FCA and on-board bill of lading** — buyer can instruct its carrier to issue an on-board B/L to the seller.
4. **Own means of transport allowed** in FCA, DAP, DPU and DDP (no third-party carrier needed).
5. **Security-related requirements and costs** allocated explicitly (A4/B4, A7/B7).
6. **Costs listed in one place** (article A9/B9) and the articles reordered (A1–A10 seller, B1–B10 buyer), with delivery and risk now at A2/A3.
7. More explanatory notes for users.

## 5. Selecting the optimal Incoterm ⭐⭐⭐
Balance **cost, risk and control**, tailored to the transport mode and the logistics expertise of each party 📘. Key factors 📘:
1. **Mode of transport** — for containerised, air or rail freight avoid maritime-only FOB/CIF; use FCA, CPT or CIP.
2. **Risk transfer vs cost transfer** — under C terms risk passes before costs (on board at origin) while the seller pays freight to destination.
3. **Control and logistics capabilities** — buyer with a strong forwarder → EXW/FCA; seller better equipped → DAP/DDP.
4. **Customs expertise** — the party more familiar with local rules should clear customs; avoid DDP if the seller doesn't know the destination's customs.
5. **Insurance requirements** — CIP (ICC A) gives higher cover than CIF (ICC C).

Recommended by scenario 📘: containerised → **FCA** (risk passes at delivery to carrier, not at loading on vessel); maximum buyer convenience → **DDP**; maximum buyer control (minimum seller obligation) → **EXW**; high-value goods → **CIP**; non-containerised sea freight/bulk → **FOB**. An exporter wanting to grow sales should offer the importer the most **customer-friendly** (D or C) quote 📘.

### Quick decision flow 📗
1. Is the cargo bulk/break-bulk loaded directly on a ship? → **FAS / FOB / CFR / CIF**. Otherwise (containers, air, road, rail, multimodal) → **EXW / FCA / CPT / CIP / DAP / DPU / DDP**.
2. Who arranges and pays the main carriage? Buyer → **F** (or E); seller → **C** or **D**.
3. If the seller pays freight, where should risk pass? At origin (seller wants low risk) → **C**; at destination (buyer wants delivered service) → **D**.
4. Who must insure? Seller (by contract) → **CIF** (sea) or **CIP** (any mode).
5. Who unloads? Seller → **DPU**.
6. Who clears import and pays duty? Seller → **DDP**; buyer → everything else.

### Common mistakes ⭐⭐
- Using **FOB or CIF for containers or air** (risk stays with seller after it has lost control; carrier receipts don't evidence "on board"). Use FCA/CPT/CIP.
- Using **EXW for exports** where the buyer cannot clear export.
- Vague places ("FOB China"); forgetting the version year.
- Thinking Incoterms transfer **ownership** or set **payment** terms.
- Choosing **DDP** without being able to register for import VAT/GST or clear customs in the buyer's country.
- Assuming CIF/CIP insurance fully protects the buyer — CIF cover is minimum (ICC C).

### Incoterms and other documents
- **Letter of credit**: C terms need an on-board B/L as evidence of shipment; FCA 2020's on-board B/L option fixes the L/C problem.
- **Customs valuation**: India assesses import duty on **CIF value** (assessable value); exports are recorded on **FOB value**.
- **Insurance**: 110% of the CIF/CIP value is the standard minimum insured sum (contract value + 10% for expected profit).

## Self-test
**Q1. What is an Incoterm and who publishes it?**
A three-letter trade term published by the International Chamber of Commerce (first in 1936, now Incoterms® 2020) that defines the tasks, costs and risks of seller and buyer in delivering goods under a sale contract, including the point where risk transfers and who handles export/import formalities.

**Q2. Classify the 11 Incoterms by mode of transport.**
Any mode (7): EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP. Sea and inland waterway (4): FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF.

**Q3. Why is FCA preferred over FOB for container shipments?**
Container cargo is handed over at an inland depot or container yard well before it is loaded on the ship. Under FOB the seller keeps the risk until the goods are on board, though it no longer controls them; under FCA risk passes when the goods reach the carrier, which matches the real hand-over. FCA also works for every mode.

**Q4. What is the difference between CIF and CIP on insurance?**
Both oblige the seller to insure for at least 110% of contract value, but CIF requires only minimum cover (Institute Cargo Clauses C, named perils) and is sea-only, while CIP (2020) requires all-risks cover (ICC A) and works for any mode.

**Q5. Which is the only Incoterm that requires the seller to unload, and what did it replace?**
DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded); it replaced DAT (Delivered at Terminal) in 2020.

**Q6. Name five things Incoterms do not cover.**
Transfer of title, payment terms and price, remedies for breach, force majeure, tariffs/sanctions/prohibitions, IP rights, dispute resolution, and whether a contract exists.

**Q7. In CFR, when do risk and cost transfer?**
Risk transfers when the goods are on board the vessel at the port of shipment; the seller pays the freight up to the named port of destination. Two critical points: risk passes at origin, cost at destination.

**Q8. Which rule places the maximum and the minimum obligation on the seller?**
Maximum: DDP (seller delivers, clears import and pays duties). Minimum: EXW (seller only makes goods available at its premises).
