## 1. The 7 steps in calculating shipping costs ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
Whatever the destination or mode (sea, air, road, rail), costs follow the same **seven stages** of the transport process:
1. **Export transport (collection)** — pick-up from the supplier to the port/airport/hub.
2. **Export customs clearance.**
3. **Goods handling in the country of origin** (terminal handling, loading, stuffing).
4. **Freight** (the main carriage).
5. **Import customs clearance.**
6. **Goods handling at destination.**
7. **Import transport (delivery)** to the consignee.
Link with Incoterms: the Incoterm decides which of these seven the seller pays and which the buyer pays.

## 2. Cost by mode ⭐⭐⭐

### Sea freight 📘
Two options: **LCL** and **FCL**.
- **LCL (Less than Container Load)**: general cargo for smaller deliveries where **several importers share a container**; best for **< 15 m³**. Freight is priced on **volume (m³)**, the space the goods take — the greater the volume, the higher the price. LCL prices vary much less, because the ocean freight is a small part of the total; the bigger costs are **collection, export and import handling, and delivery** (consolidation at CFS, deconsolidation).
- **FCL (Full Container Load)**: better when shipping **> 15 m³** — you fill the whole container. FCL price varies considerably with **supply and demand** (half-empty ships on a route → lower price, and vice versa), and with exchange rates, oil prices, traffic, war-risk charges and seasonality.
- **Pick-up location** matters: collection several hundred km inland in China costs much more than from a coastal city with its own port (Shanghai).

### Air freight 📘
Chosen when **speed** is crucial; transit typically **3–12 days** door to door depending on service level and origin. Two types: **standard air freight** and **courier services**.
- Standard air freight is priced on **volumetric (dimensional) weight** using the airline's **DIM factor**: volume in cm³ ÷ **6,000** = kg, i.e. about **166.67 kg per m³**. Example: 1 m³ weighing 100 kg → chargeable **167 kg**; 1 m³ weighing 500 kg → chargeable **500 kg** (actual weight is higher).
- Price also depends on the **route** (supply and demand), the forwarder's **negotiating power** on volume, **currency**, **fuel surcharges** and **transit time** (faster = dearer).

### Sea vs air rule of thumb ⭐⭐ 📘
If the shipment is **< 0.5 m³ and < 100 kg**, air may be competitive, because **sea freight has a minimum chargeable volume of 1 m³**. Above **0.5 m³ and 100 kg**, sea is generally cheaper — but transit can be **up to 43 days** from places like China.

### Road freight 📘
- **LTL (Less than Truckload)**: typically **1–2 pallets**; you buy pallet spaces and share the truck with other importers. A small truck collects → hub warehouse → larger export truck → local warehouse at destination → small truck delivers.
- **FTL (Full Truckload)**: you buy the whole truck — typically **33 pallet spaces or about 90 m³** for a standard curtain-sider. No handling or trans-shipment cost, as the truck runs **directly from supplier to you**; slightly shorter transit.
- Consignments (groupage) also exist. Forwarders have **fixed LTL rates by volume, weight and distance**; each country is divided into **zones**, and price depends on start and end zones; plus **fuel surcharges** and special handling charges.

### Rail freight 📘
**Cheaper than air, faster than sea, greener than road** (e.g. China–Europe rail). Costs depend on destination and demand; you need the cargo size and total volume; compare total freight cost against the cost per m³ to find the cheapest mode.

## 3. Why ocean freight cost calculation matters ⭐⭐ 📘
Ocean freight directly affects **retail prices, product margins and delivery schedules**; bad estimates cause unexpected charges, customs issues and damaged supplier relationships. **UNCTAD's Review of Maritime Transport 2024**: ocean freight costs rose **15–25%** globally between 2020 and 2023 (port congestion, fuel inflation, climate-related delays). Especially critical for **SMEs**, **JIT retailers** and **e-commerce brands using DDP**. Freight pricing also affects **trade equity** — developing nations often pay more per unit because of inefficient port access or container imbalances.

## 4. FCL rate types ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
| Rate type | Description | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Spot** | One-time price set for a specific shipment at a particular moment | Varies with vessel space, market demand and current conditions |
| **By validity period** | Rate agreed for a set period (weeks to a year) | Good for regular shippers who want to avoid volatility |
| **Contract** | Agreement between customer and line on a rate for a **determined volume** over an agreed period | Price stability for the customer; assured business for the line |
| **All-in** | Includes all costs of transport, at various service levels (possibly door-to-door) | Simple, transparent single rate; easy planning and budgeting |

## 5. Actors in maritime freight rates ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
| Actor | Description | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **Carriers (shipping lines)** | Operate the cargo ships | Provide sea transport; **control space supply and set freight rates**; responsible for cargo safety and timely arrival |
| **Non-Vessel Operators (NVOs)** | Intermediaries that do **not own ships** | Link shippers and lines; negotiate rates, coordinate transport, manage documentation |
| **NVOCC (Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier)** | Like NVOs but **issue their own bills of lading** and take more responsibility for cargo | Integrated services: consolidation, tracking, customs; **buy space from lines and resell under their own B/L** |
| **Freight forwarders** | Organise and coordinate international shipments | Multimodal (sea, land, air); negotiate rates; prepare export documents; arrange customs clearance at destination |

## 6. Factors that influence maritime freight rates ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
**Economic**: supply and demand of ships and equipment (containers, chassis); **peak season** (holiday rush raises rates); **global economic cycles** (recessions lower demand and rates).
**Logistic**: **operating costs** (fuel, labour, maintenance, security); **itineraries, distances and navigation routes**; **port infrastructure** (efficiency and capacity affect transit time and cost); **loading/unloading capacity**.
**Regulatory and political**: **government regulations** (safety, environment); **international trade agreements** (shift trade flows); **geopolitical conflicts** (route disruption, higher insurance).
**Environmental**: **emission regulations** (cleaner tech investment); **weather** (storms, hurricanes); **technological innovations** (fuel-efficient vessels, fleet management can lower costs).

### Other factors (12) ⭐⭐ 📘
1. **Fuel prices** — bunker fuel is a major cost; carriers add fuel surcharges when oil surges.
2. **Port fees** — docking, handling, storage; vary by port efficiency, infrastructure and location; congested or modernising ports charge more.
3. **Surcharges** — BAF (fuel), CAF (currency), **emergency risk surcharge** (piracy, war), **peak season surcharge**.
4. **Currency exchange rates.**
5. **Demand and supply dynamics** (peaks raise rates; overcapacity lowers them).
6. **Environmental regulations** — IMO sulphur cap (IMO 2020: 0.5% sulphur) → costlier fuels passed on.
7. **Geopolitical and economic factors** — rerouting, longer transit, security.
8. **Insurance** — varies with nature and value of goods.
9. **Container handling and haulage** — moving boxes to/from ports and loading them is a big share of cost.
10. **Carrier charges** — container use, documentation, booking fees: the foundational costs.
11. **Type and size of container** — dry boxes cheapest; reefers, flat racks cost more; larger containers cheaper per unit for bulk goods.
12. **Special handling** — hazardous or special cargo needs extra procedures, equipment and precautions.

## 7. Surcharges and fees — know every abbreviation ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
| Code | Name | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **BAF** | Bunker Adjustment Factor | Fluctuating **fuel** costs; updated monthly or quarterly |
| **CAF** | Currency Adjustment Factor | **Exchange-rate** risk, e.g. USD contracts with EUR/RMB suppliers; often a % of base freight (+BAF) |
| **THC** | Terminal Handling Charge | Loading/unloading and equipment handling at **origin and/or destination** terminals |
| **PSS** | Peak Season Surcharge | High-demand seasons, e.g. **August–October** for holiday goods |
| **EBS** | Emergency Bunker Surcharge | Fuel crises/geopolitical disruptions (Red Sea crisis, Suez delays) |
| **GRI** | General Rate Increase | Temporary carrier-announced price hikes on volatile lanes |
| **Green / carbon fees** | Sustainability fees (2025+) | IMO 2023 rules, **CII** (Carbon Intensity Indicator), EU ETS — green premiums for cleaner fuel/retrofits |
| **ISPS** | International Ship and Port Facility Security charge | Port/ship security (ISPS Code) |
| Others | Documentation/B/L fee, CFS charges, war-risk/emergency risk surcharge, CIC (container imbalance), transhipment fee | |

### Key components of ocean freight charges ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
1. **Base ocean freight rate** — core charge port to port; quoted **per container for FCL** and **per ton or per m³ for LCL**. Depends on route (Asia–Europe vs Trans-Pacific), vessel type and space, container size (20', 40', high cube) and seasonality. Published via forwarders, NVOCCs, or portals (Maersk Spot, Hapag-Lloyd Quick Quotes).
2. **Surcharges** — BAF/fuel, security surcharge, PSS.
3. **Handling charges** — loading and unloading along the chain (THC).
4. **Additional costs** — CAF, CFS charges.
5. **Additional service-based costs** — pre-shipment inspection, customs clearance, inland haulage, cargo insurance, last-mile delivery.

**Total freight charges = base freight + surcharges + additional costs** 📘.

## 8. Methods of calculating freight rates ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
| # | Method | Formula | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Per mile / per km** | Rate per mile × total distance | Trucking, road |
| 2 | **Weight-based** | Rate per unit weight × total weight | Air and rail |
| 3 | **Volume-based (dimensional weight)** | DIM weight (lb) = L × W × H (in) ÷ DIM factor; charge on the higher of DIM and actual weight | Light, bulky parcels |
| 4 | **Flat rate** | One fixed charge regardless of distance/weight | Specific routes/services |
| 5 | **Zone-based** | Rate for the origin–destination zone pair | USPS, couriers |
| 6 | **Freight class (LTL)** | Density (lb/ft³) = weight ÷ (L × W × H in ft) → NMFC class **50–500** | US LTL carriers |
- DIM factor: **139 in³/lb domestic**, **166 in³/lb international** (FedEx SmartPost, DHL use 166) 📘. Conversions: 1 lb = 0.453 kg; 1 inch = 0.0254 m 📘.
- Freight class is based on **density, stowability, handling and liability** 📘.

## 9. How to calculate ocean freight charges (LCL) — 5 steps ⭐⭐⭐ 📘
1. **Weight and dimensions**: package volume (m³) = L × W × H (cm) ÷ 1,000,000; total volume = package volume × number of packages (add them if sizes differ); total weight likewise.
2. **Chargeable weight** = the **greater** of actual weight and **volumetric weight**, where volumetric weight = volume (m³) × carrier's conversion factor (kg/m³). (Note: when total weight exceeds **1,000 kg**, actual weight is typically used for LCL.)
3. **Research freight rates** — base rate per kg or per m³ plus port handling and fuel surcharges.
4. **Base freight** = chargeable weight × base rate. **For ocean freight the conversion factor is 1,000 kg per m³ (1 m³ = 1 tonne)** — the **W/M (weight or measure)** or **revenue ton** rule.
5. **Total freight** = base freight + surcharges + additional costs; confirm with a quote.

### Tips to save on ocean freight 📘
Gather correct shipment details (weight, volume, type, dimensions, origin/destination, FCL vs LCL); obtain and compare several quotes (or use a digital forwarder); **consolidate** shipments; **plan early** (avoid urgent, expensive routes); **leverage technology** (forwarder software).

### Real-time quote platforms (2025) 📘
**Freightos** (instant FCL/LCL), **Flexport** (digital forwarding with breakdowns), **Maersk Spot**, **Shipwaves** (emerging markets), **Cogoport** (AI-enhanced lanes, predictive pricing); often with analytics, carbon calculators and customs support.

## 10. Technologies affecting freight costs (2023–25) ⭐⭐ 📘
1. **Digital twins and AI forecasting** (Singapore, Antwerp) — forecast dwell time and congestion → more accurate pricing.
2. **Blockchain smart contracts** (TradeLens, GSBN) — faster, tamper-proof billing, fewer errors/fraud.
3. **Greener fuels and ESG metrics** — under IMO, **EU Fit for 55** and **BIMCO**: biofuel blends, methanol, wind-assisted propulsion; costs passed to shippers as carbon fees.

## 11. Evolution of maritime freight rates ⭐⭐ 📘
- **Pre-pandemic** — relatively stable with seasonal swings; **strategic alliances** kept balance and avoided overcapacity.
- **US–China trade war (2018)** — tariffs restructured supply chains; firms shifted outside China, raising demand and rates on other routes.
- **Pandemic (2020–21)** — demand drop and lockdowns first cut rates; then the consumer goods boom caused capacity shortages and **record rates**.
- **Post-pandemic 2022–23** — rates stabilised but stayed high: disruptions, container shortages, port labour shortages, rising operating costs.
- **Russia–Ukraine conflict (2022)** — sanctions and Black Sea uncertainty; diversion to longer, safer routes raised costs.
- **Red Sea crisis (2024)** — Houthi attacks cut traffic through **Bab el-Mandeb and Suez**; ships diverted via the **Cape of Good Hope**, adding time and cost.
- **Sustainability** — stricter rules and cleaner fuels raise costs; **EU ETS** for shipping adds cost passed to consumers.

### Alliances (additional reading) 📘
Alliances let lines optimise routes and share capacity. Example: **2M alliance (Maersk + MSC, 2015)** shaped rate structures through service consolidation and shared capacity, lowering operating costs; **dissolution** of alliances (2M ended in 2025) can create uncertainty and price swings.

## Self-test
**Q1. List the seven cost stages in any shipment.**
Export transport (collection), export customs clearance, handling at origin, freight, import customs clearance, handling at destination, import transport (delivery).

**Q2. When should a shipper choose LCL over FCL?**
For shipments under about 15 m³; LCL shares a container and is priced per m³ (W/M), while FCL is better above 15 m³ because you pay for the whole box but avoid consolidation handling.

**Q3. What is chargeable weight?**
The weight used to price the shipment: the greater of actual gross weight and volumetric (dimensional) weight. For air, volumetric kg = cm³ ÷ 6,000 (167 kg/m³); for ocean LCL, 1 m³ = 1,000 kg.

**Q4. Explain BAF and CAF.**
BAF (Bunker Adjustment Factor) adjusts freight for fuel price changes; CAF (Currency Adjustment Factor) compensates for exchange-rate fluctuations, often as a percentage of base freight plus BAF.

**Q5. Differentiate NVOCC and freight forwarder.**
An NVOCC buys space from lines and resells it under its own bill of lading, acting as a carrier to the shipper; a freight forwarder arranges and coordinates shipments as the shipper's agent across modes, negotiating rates, preparing documents and arranging clearance.

**Q6. What are the four FCL rate types?**
Spot, by validity period, contract (volume-based) and all-in.

**Q7. Why did freight rates spike in 2021 and again in 2024?**
2021: post-COVID demand surge for goods with congestion and container shortages; 2024: Red Sea/Houthi attacks forced Suez traffic around the Cape of Good Hope, adding time, fuel and insurance.
