## 1. Outsourcing & contracts ⭐⭐⭐
- **Outsourcing** = who (third party). **Offshoring** = where (another country). Captive offshoring = own unit abroad. **Nearshoring** = nearby country (US→Mexico, EU→Poland).
- **Why outsource**: cost, core focus, capability/technology, flexibility, speed, risk sharing, capital release. **Risks**: control loss, hidden costs, quality, IP, lock-in, compliance, currency/political, ethics.
- **1PL** shipper · **2PL** carrier · **3PL** contract logistics · **4PL** integrator managing 3PLs · **5PL** network aggregator.
- **Weighted score** = Σ(weight × rating) ÷ 100 → rank → **sensitivity** (re-scale weights to 100).
- **Total cost** = contract value + **hidden costs** (audit, travel, ERP integration, QC, contract management, transition, exit).
- **Risk score** = Probability × Impact (1–5 each): 15–25 high, 8–14 medium, 1–7 low. 4 Ts: Treat, Transfer, Tolerate, Terminate.
- **SLA element**: KPI + target + measurement + reporting + penalty + incentive + exclusions + escalation.
- **Clauses**: SOW, price/indexation, SLA, **liquidated damages** (pre-estimate, s.74), **force majeure** (beyond control, notice, mitigation, suspension), **termination** (cause + cure period; convenience), warranties/indemnity, limitation of liability, NDA/IP, **audit rights**, code of conduct/ESG, BCP, change control, dispute resolution (arbitration), exit, step-in.
- **BATNA** = best alternative to a negotiated agreement; ZOPA = overlap of reservation prices; interests not positions.
- **Single vs multi-sourcing**: cost & partnership vs resilience & flexibility.
- **Payback** = one-off cost ÷ annual saving. Que 12: Year-1 cost $12.0 m vs $14.3 m → save $2.3 m; later $2.9 m/yr (+$0.55 m productivity); payback ≈ 2.5 months.

## 2. Incoterms® 2020 ⭐⭐⭐
- ICC, **1936**; revised 1953, 67, 76, 80, 90, 2000, 2010, **2020**. Answer: tasks, costs, **risk transfer**. Not: title, payment, price, breach remedies, FM, IP, tariffs, sanctions, disputes.
- **Any mode (7)**: EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP. **Sea only (4)**: FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF.
- **E** departure · **F** main carriage unpaid · **C** main carriage paid (risk at origin — two critical points) · **D** arrival.
- Risk points: EXW seller's premises · FCA to buyer's carrier · FAS alongside ship · FOB on board · CFR/CIF on board at origin · CPT/CIP first carrier · DAP at destination ready for unloading · **DPU unloaded** · DDP destination, duty paid.
- Insurance: **CIF = ICC (C)**, **CIP = ICC (A)**, both **110%**. Others: no obligation.
- Export clearance: buyer only in **EXW**. Import clearance & duty: seller only in **DDP**.
- 2020 changes: DAT→**DPU**; CIP to ICC (A); FCA on-board B/L; own transport; security costs; costs listed in A9/B9.
- Best: containers → **FCA**; high value → **CIP**; bulk → **FOB**; max buyer convenience → **DDP**; max buyer control → **EXW**. Never FOB/CIF for air or containers.
- Format: *FCA Warehouse 3, Shanghai, Incoterms® 2020*.
- Cases: vaccines **DAP** · machinery **FCA** · coffee **FOB** · engine parts **CIF** · garments **CPT** · laptops **CIP** · agri machinery **DPU** · solar **DDP** · grapes **CFR** · chemicals **EXW** · Problem 11: **CIF / DAP / DDP** · Problem 12: FOB wrong → **FCA/CIP**.

## 3. Containerization & imbalance ⭐⭐⭐
- ISO-standard box; intermodal = one load unit, many modes. McLean, *Ideal X*, 1956.
- **20'**: 33.2 m³, 28,180 kg, 5.9 × 2.35 × 2.4 m. **40'**: 67.7 m³, 28,750 kg, 12.03 × 2.35 × 2.4 m. TEU/FEU.
- **Objectives (7)**: weather protection, standard box, easy loading, temporary storage, anti-theft, crane hooks, less handling.
- **Types**: general, thermal (reefer/insulated/ventilated), dry, flat rack, bulk, garment, tank, gas, open top, car carrier, special, open side, double door, tunnel, half-height.
- **Imbalance**: cargo but no boxes here, idle empties there. Stockpiling: late orders, cash flow, storage space.
- **Causes**: high rates, space, one forwarder, cargo weight, container shortage (+ trade imbalance, storage cost, time pressure, Excel planning, no cooperation).
- **Effects**: late orders/cash flow, higher costs, delays, environment, unreliable commerce, inventory shortages, contract penalties.
- **CAx**: 0.5 balanced; >0.5 surplus (more full in); <0.5 shortage (more full out).
- **ECR facts**: 56% of life idle/empty; 170 m shipped, 50 m empty; 10% assets empty; 20.5% port handlings; $15–20 bn/yr; 5 free days then ~$100/day; 75% empty US/EU→Asia in 2020–21.
- **CIC**: surcharge for repositioning; consignee pays if it receives more than it ships; shipper if it ships more.
- **Solutions**: digitalisation, forecasts, CAx, ECR, virtual pools, **SOC**, street turns, sharing, substitution, connectainers, foldable (4-in-5, 6-in-1, −75% space).
- Demurrage (inside terminal) vs detention (outside).

## 4. Maritime & logistics costing ⭐⭐⭐
- **7 steps**: export transport, export clearance, origin handling, freight, import clearance, destination handling, import transport.
- LCL < 15 m³ (priced per m³); FCL > 15 m³ (supply–demand volatile). Sea minimum 1 m³; air may win if < 0.5 m³ and < 100 kg.
- Road: LTL 1–2 pallets; **FTL 33 pallets ≈ 90 m³**. Rail: cheaper than air, faster than sea, greener than road.
- Rate types: **spot, validity period, contract, all-in**. Actors: carriers, NVOs, **NVOCC** (own B/L), forwarders.
- Surcharges: **BAF** fuel, **CAF** currency, **THC** terminal, **PSS** peak (Aug–Oct), **EBS** emergency bunker, **GRI**, green/CII fees, ISPS, documentation.
- Methods: per mile, weight, DIM (÷139 domestic / ÷166 intl, inches→lb), flat, zone, freight class (density; NMFC 50–500).
- Ocean: chargeable = max(actual, volume × factor); **1 m³ = 1,000 kg** (W/M). Total = base + surcharges + additional.
- Examples: 2×500 = $1,000 · 0.5×2,000 = $1,000 · 24×18×12/139 = 37.2 lb · 500/64 = 7.81 lb/ft³ · 30×200 = 6,000 kg · 125×50 = $6,250 · Canada–US $20,000 · Shanghai–LA **$2,966** (CAF 5% of 2,280 = $114).
- UNCTAD 2024: freight +15–25% (2020–23). Events: trade war 2018, COVID, Russia–Ukraine 2022, **Red Sea 2024** (Cape of Good Hope). 2M = Maersk + MSC (2015).

## 5. Air freight pricing & security ⭐⭐⭐
- Measure to extreme points; odd shapes = enclosing box; protrusions charged in full.
- **6 m³ = 1 t; 6,000 cm³ = 1 kg; 1 m³ = 166.67 kg**; some use 5,000. Chargeable = higher, **rounded up to next 0.5 kg**.
- Example: 150 kg, 120×80×50 → 80 kg vol → **150 kg** chargeable.
- Minimum charge; rate per kg falls with weight; fuel & war-risk surcharges; origin and destination extras.
- **Montreal 1999**: **22 SDR/kg** (1 SDR = $1.43493 → **$31.57/kg**) → insure door-to-door.
- Risks: bombs, undeclared DG, contraband, theft, hijack/sabotage. Controls: screening, facility security, **known shipper**, training, access control, tamper seals, blast-resistant ULDs, biometrics.
- **EU Reg 831/06 (2007)** client info + audits · UK known vs unknown cargo · **2010 Yemen bomb** · **TSA 2012** 100% screening on passenger aircraft · EU screening at origin 2012 · **ACC3 2014** · US–EU mutual recognition 2012.

## 6. Insurance & General Average ⭐⭐⭐
- Interests: ship (H&M), cargo (ICC), freight; liabilities (P&I).
- Principles: utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, subrogation, contribution, proximate cause.
- **ICC A** all risks · **B** + earthquake, washing overboard, sea water entry · **C** fire, sinking, stranding, collision, jettison, GA. Exclusions: packing, inherent vice, delay, wilful misconduct, war/strikes unless added.
- Losses: total (actual, constructive) and partial (**PA** accidental, own loss; **GA** intentional, common safety, shared).
- **GA conditions**: real imminent common peril · extraordinary · intentional · reasonable · common safety · success · direct consequence.
- **York-Antwerp Rules** (1864 … **2016**) via B/L and policies. Adjuster: GA bond + guarantee/deposit, values, rate, statement.
- **GA rate = GA loss ÷ CV**; contribution = value × rate; net = made good − contribution (sum = 0).
- Answers: **Q1** 5% (ship 450k, elec 125k, text 75k, furn 50k; elec net +175k) · **Q2** 4.5833% (wheat net +167,500) · **Q3** 3.75% (ship 375k, mach 112.5k, chem 75k, steel 37.5k) · **Q4** 7.931% (crude pays net 46,551.72) · **Q5** 7% (cars net +80k) · **Q6** 1.3333% → cargo ₹9,33,333 · **Q7** 1.0909% → cargo ₹19,63,636, ship ₹13,09,091 · **Q9** claim ₹28,22,400, uninsured ₹12,09,600.

## 7. Air transportation ⭐⭐⭐
- Services: express, standard, deferred. Direct routing = flexibility/speed; indirect = cheapest, remote reach.
- AAI (Department of Cargo); **IATA** — non-political commercial regulator; 290 airlines, 400 partners, 100,000 agents.
- **ULD** = containers of the skies. **AWB** (airline contract, limits liability, non-negotiable) · **HAWB** (forwarder/IATA agent) · **MAWB** (forwarder ↔ airline).
- Hubs & spokes (high unit cost). **CASS**: one payment a month.
- Advantages: speed, reliability, accuracy, control, scheduling. Disadvantages: risk, high cost, limited capacity, weather, unfit products.
- Air = **4–5× road, 12–16× sea**.
- Industry: IATA, airlines, lessors, cargo agents, airport authorities.
- Price factors: **density, quantity, distance, shape, type**.
- Services (6): air mail, express, scheduled, charter, leased (dry/damp/wet/**ACMI**), forwarder.
- Aircraft: passenger (belly), **quick-change**, **combi**, freighter.
- 15 steps: supplier → Incoterms → forwarder → documents → volumetric ratio → pack (security) → route → documentation → AWB → transit → pre-arrival clearance → arrival → CTO stores under bond → collection → delivery.
- 2019: FedEx no. 1 airline (17,503 m FTK); Hong Kong no. 1 airport (4.8 m t). IATA benefits: errors −87%, fines 81%, customs delays 79%, aircraft damage 79%, speed 70%.
- **IEC**: DGFT, 10-digit = PAN, update yearly.
