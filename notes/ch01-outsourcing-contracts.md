## 1. What outsourcing is ⭐⭐⭐
**Outsourcing** is contracting an external provider to perform an activity (a process, a service or the manufacture of a component) that the firm could do, or used to do, in-house. Ownership of the *activity* moves out of the firm; accountability for the *outcome* to the customer does not.

**Offshoring** is moving an activity to another country. It is about **location**, not ownership.

| | Same country | Another country |
|---|---|---|
| **Done in-house (own entity)** | In-house / insourcing | **Captive offshoring** (own subsidiary abroad, e.g. own development centre in Vietnam) |
| **Done by a third party** | **Domestic (onshore) outsourcing** (e.g. hire another Indian IT firm) | **Offshore outsourcing** (third party abroad) |

- **Nearshoring**: moving work to a nearby country (same time zone/region), e.g. US → Mexico, Western Europe → Poland. Shorter lead time, lower freight, lower geopolitical and currency risk, higher unit cost than far-shore.
- **Reshoring / backshoring**: bringing work back home. **Friend-shoring**: sourcing from politically aligned countries (China+1).
- **Insourcing**: bringing a previously outsourced activity back in-house.

### Why firms outsource ⭐⭐
1. **Cost** — labour arbitrage, supplier's economies of scale, variable instead of fixed cost.
2. **Focus on core competence** (Prahalad & Hamel) — keep what differentiates you, outsource the rest.
3. **Access to capability and technology** that the firm lacks (cold chain, WMS, customs expertise).
4. **Flexibility / scalability** — capacity up or down with demand (peak season).
5. **Speed to market** and **global reach** (3PL networks, freight forwarders with offices worldwide).
6. **Risk sharing** — the provider carries assets and some operational risk.
7. **Capital release** — no investment in warehouses, trucks, IT (asset-light).

### Risks and disadvantages ⭐⭐
Loss of control and visibility; **hidden costs** (see §5); quality failure; IP leakage; dependence/lock-in; communication, culture and time-zone gaps; regulatory non-compliance; currency and political risk; reputational/ethical risk (child labour, unsafe factories); hollowing-out of skills; transition disruption; supplier failure.

### Make-or-buy (in-house vs outsource) decision factors
Strategic importance (core vs non-core), total cost (not just price), capability and capacity, quality, control of IP, volume stability, supplier market (number of capable suppliers), risk, time to implement, and reversibility.

## 2. Outsourcing in logistics: 1PL to 5PL ⭐⭐
| Level | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **1PL** | The shipper itself | Own trucks and warehouses |
| **2PL** | Asset-based carrier | Moves goods: shipping line, airline, trucking company, railway |
| **3PL** | Third-party logistics provider | Integrated, contract-based services: warehousing, transport, freight forwarding, customs, value-added services (kitting, labelling). E.g. DHL Supply Chain, TCI, Mahindra Logistics |
| **4PL** | Lead logistics provider / integrator | Manages the whole supply chain and the 3PLs on the client's behalf; usually non-asset, information and management-led (Accenture concept) |
| **5PL** | Network aggregator | Plans and runs the supply network for e-commerce/multiple clients, aggregating demand across chains |

Other logistics intermediaries: **freight forwarder** (arranges carriage, documents, consolidation), **NVOCC** (issues own bill of lading, buys space from lines), **customs broker / CHA**, **IATA cargo agent** (issues HAWB).

## 3. The outsourcing process (life cycle) ⭐⭐
1. **Strategic assessment** — what to outsource and why; build the business case (TCO, risk).
2. **Specification** — scope of work, service levels, volumes, KPIs.
3. **Supplier search & shortlisting** — RFI → RFP/RFQ.
4. **Supplier evaluation & selection** — weighted scoring, site audits, references.
5. **Negotiation & contracting** — price, SLA, penalties/incentives, clauses.
6. **Transition** — knowledge transfer, IT integration, parallel run.
7. **Relationship & performance management** — KPIs, scorecards, governance meetings, audits.
8. **Renewal, re-tender or exit** — exit plan, re-insourcing.

## 4. Supplier / vendor selection: the weighted scoring model ⭐⭐⭐
Steps (used in Problem Q1 and Q9):
1. Choose criteria (cost, quality, delivery, technology, sustainability, risk…).
2. Assign **weights** that add up to 100% (or 100 points) to reflect strategy.
3. Rate each supplier on each criterion (e.g. 1–10).
4. **Weighted score = Σ (weight × rating)**; divide by 100 if weights are in points.
5. Rank; choose the highest; run a **sensitivity analysis** (change a weight and see if the ranking flips).

> When one weight increases, the others must be re-scaled so that the total stays 100. Either reduce them **proportionally** or take the increase from one named criterion; state which you did.

Other selection tools: **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)**, **total landed cost** (price + freight + duty/tariff + insurance + inventory in transit + quality cost), **AHP** (pairwise comparison), **categorical/rating method**, **Kraljic matrix** (profit impact × supply risk → leverage, strategic, bottleneck, non-critical items).

### Total landed cost ⭐⭐⭐
**Landed cost per unit = unit price + freight + insurance + duties/tariffs + customs/clearance + handling + in-transit inventory carrying cost + cost of quality (defects, rework)**

In-transit carrying cost ≈ unit value × carrying-cost rate × (lead time ÷ 365). A low unit price far away can lose to a nearshore supplier once tariff, lead time, quality and risk are priced in.

## 5. Hidden costs of outsourcing ⭐⭐⭐
Costs that are not in the supplier's contract price but arise *because* you outsourced:
- **Search and selection** — RFP, supplier audits, travel.
- **Transition** — knowledge transfer, parallel running, severance, ERP/EDI integration.
- **Governance / contract management** — vendor managers, review meetings, legal.
- **Quality assurance** — incoming inspection, audits, rework, returns.
- **Coordination & communication** — travel, time-zone overlap, translation.
- **Risk costs** — buffer inventory, expediting, dual sourcing, insurance.
- **Exit / switching** costs.

Why they are ignored: they are spread across many departments' budgets; they occur after the decision; procurement is rewarded on price; they are hard to estimate; vendors' proposals only show price. **Total outsourcing cost = contract value + hidden costs**; hidden costs of 5–15% of contract value are common.

## 6. Outsourcing risk assessment: the risk matrix ⭐⭐⭐
**Risk score = Probability × Impact** (each typically rated 1–5). Plot on a 5×5 heat map:
- **High (15–25)** — red: mitigate immediately, possibly avoid.
- **Medium (8–14)** — amber: mitigate and monitor.
- **Low (1–7)** — green: accept and monitor.

Four responses (the **4 Ts**): **Treat/mitigate** (controls, audits, dual sourcing), **Transfer** (contract clauses, insurance, hedging), **Tolerate/accept**, **Terminate/avoid** (don't outsource that activity).

What can be **transferred by contract**: quality failure (warranty, rejection & replacement at supplier's cost, liquidated damages), delay (LDs, penalty), regulatory non-compliance (compliance warranty + indemnity), IP leakage (NDA, IP ownership & indemnity clause, audit rights). Currency risk is transferred with a **currency clause** (price in buyer's currency, or a band/adjustment) or hedged with forwards. A strike is usually **force majeure** — transferred back to the buyer unless the contract excludes it, so mitigate with dual sourcing.

## 7. Service Level Agreements (SLAs) and KPIs ⭐⭐⭐
An **SLA** is the part of the contract that defines the measurable level of service, how it is measured, and what happens if it is missed or beaten. A good SLA element has:
1. **KPI and definition** (formula, what counts).
2. **Target** (and sometimes a minimum floor).
3. **Measurement method** — data source, frequency, who measures (WMS report, audit, cycle count, customer portal).
4. **Reporting** — monthly dashboard, quarterly business review.
5. **Penalty / service credit** for missing the target (e.g. 1% of monthly fee per 0.1% shortfall, capped).
6. **Incentive / bonus** for exceeding it (gain-share).
7. **Exclusions** (force majeure, buyer-caused delays) and **escalation**.

Good KPIs are **SMART** (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound) and few (5–10). Balance **cost, quality, time, flexibility, safety, customer**.

Common warehouse/transport KPIs (formulas):
| KPI | Formula | Typical target |
|---|---|---|
| Order accuracy | Correct orders ÷ total orders shipped × 100 | ≥ 99–99.5% |
| Inventory accuracy | Correct records ÷ records counted × 100 | ≥ 99.5% |
| Damage rate | Damaged orders ÷ orders shipped × 100 | ≤ 0.5% |
| Complaint rate | Complaints ÷ orders × 100 | ≤ 0.2% |
| Order fulfilment (dock-to-ship) time | Average hours from order release to dispatch | ≤ 24 h |
| On-time delivery | On-time deliveries ÷ total deliveries | ≥ 95–98% |
| OTIF | Orders on time and in full ÷ total orders | ≥ 95% |
| Perfect order | % orders on time × in full × damage-free × correct documents | ≥ 90–95% |
| Safety (LTIFR) | Lost-time injuries × 1,000,000 ÷ hours worked | → 0 |

See also [Transportation KPIs & Scorecards](ch08-transportation-kpis-scorecards.md).

## 8. The outsourcing contract: key clauses ⭐⭐⭐
| Clause | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Scope of work / statement of work** | Exactly what will be delivered |
| **Price & payment terms** | Rates, indexation (fuel, currency), invoicing, payment days |
| **SLA & KPIs** | Targets, measurement, service credits, bonuses |
| **Liquidated damages (LDs)** | A **pre-agreed, genuine pre-estimate** of loss payable for a defined breach (e.g. ₹X per day of delay). No need to prove actual loss; must not be a "penalty" (in India: Indian Contract Act s.74 — reasonable compensation up to the named amount) |
| **Force majeure** | Excuses a party from performance (usually suspension, not damages) when an event **beyond its reasonable control**, unforeseeable and unavoidable (war, natural disaster, epidemic, government lockdown, sometimes strikes) prevents performance; requires **prompt notice**, proof and mitigation; often allows termination if it lasts > X days |
| **Termination** | For cause (material breach not cured within a **cure period**, insolvency, repeated SLA failure), for convenience (with notice and fee), and on prolonged force majeure |
| **Warranties & indemnities** | Supplier promises quality/compliance; indemnifies buyer for third-party claims, IP infringement, regulatory fines |
| **Limitation of liability** | Caps damages (e.g. 12 months' fees); excludes indirect loss |
| **Confidentiality / NDA & IP** | Ownership of designs, data, improvements; no reuse |
| **Audit rights** | Buyer can inspect sites, books, compliance (quality, ESG, labour) |
| **Code of conduct / ESG clause** | No child or forced labour, safety, environment, anti-bribery; breach is material |
| **Business continuity / disaster recovery** | Backup sites, dual sourcing, inventory buffers |
| **Change control** | How scope and price changes are agreed |
| **Governance & escalation** | Review meetings, escalation ladder |
| **Dispute resolution & governing law** | Negotiation → mediation → arbitration (e.g. Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996, SIAC/ICC), seat and law |
| **Exit / transition-out** | Hand-back of data, assets, staff; transition support |
| **Step-in rights** | Buyer (or nominee) can take over the service temporarily if the supplier fails |
| **Insurance** | Supplier carries goods-in-transit, liability, cargo insurance |

### Breach of contract: what you need before claiming damages
Proof of the **contract term**; proof of the **breach** (delivery records, GRNs, emails, ASN vs receipt); proof of **notice** to the supplier and opportunity to cure; proof of **loss** and **causation** (downtime logs, emergency purchase invoices, customer penalty notices); evidence you **mitigated** the loss; and that no force-majeure or buyer-caused excuse applies. Remedies: LDs or damages (Hadley v Baxendale — losses arising naturally or in the parties' contemplation), specific performance (rare), termination.

## 9. Negotiation: BATNA, ZOPA and trade-offs ⭐⭐
- **BATNA** (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement — Fisher & Ury, *Getting to Yes*): what you will do if this deal fails. The stronger your BATNA, the more power you have.
- **Reservation price (walk-away point)**: worst acceptable term.
- **ZOPA** (zone of possible agreement): overlap between the two reservation prices.
- **Principled negotiation**: separate people from the problem; focus on **interests, not positions**; invent options for mutual gain; insist on objective criteria.
- **Trade-offs / logrolling**: give on what you value less (e.g. volume commitment) to get what you value more (price, speed). Typical levers in freight contracts: price, transit time, volume commitment, contract length, payment terms, fuel/BAF indexation, penalties and bonuses, equipment guarantee, peak-season capacity.

## 10. Sourcing strategies: single vs multiple ⭐⭐
| | Single sourcing | Multi-sourcing (dual/triple) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Lowest (volume discounts, one relationship) | Higher (split volumes, more management) |
| Quality | Consistent, deep collaboration | Variation between suppliers |
| Relationship | Strategic partnership, joint development | Competitive, arm's-length |
| Risk | **High** — single point of failure (earthquake, fire, strike, sanctions) | **Low** — disruption at one supplier covered by others |
| Flexibility | Limited by one supplier's capacity | Flexible capacity |

Hybrids: **dual sourcing** (e.g. 70/30 split), **parallel sourcing** (different parts single-sourced to different suppliers who can back each other up), single supplier with **multiple plants in different countries**. **Resilience** = ability to absorb and recover from disruption; multi-sourcing, buffers, nearshoring, visibility and flexible contracts build it. Classic lessons: Toyota/Aisin brake-valve fire (1997), Ericsson vs Nokia after the Philips Albuquerque fire (2000), Japan earthquake 2011 (Renesas chips), COVID-19 (2020–21).

## 11. Ethics and sustainability in outsourcing ⭐⭐
The buyer is responsible for its supply chain even when work is outsourced (Rana Plaza 2013, Nike sweatshops 1990s, cobalt mining). Tools: **supplier code of conduct**, **ESG clauses**, **audit rights** (announced and unannounced, third-party SA8000/SMETA/WRAP audits), **corrective action plans (CAPs)**, **supplier development** (training, capability building, fair pricing so the supplier can comply), **grievance mechanisms**, and **traceability**. Frameworks: UN Guiding Principles on Business & Human Rights, ILO conventions (C138 minimum age, C182 worst forms of child labour), India's Child and Adolescent Labour Act 1986 (as amended 2016), EU CSDDD, the Indian BRSR reporting. Immediate termination can push children into worse work; responsible exit or **remediation** (remove child safely, pay schooling, employ an adult family member) is the recommended path unless the supplier refuses to cooperate.

## 12. Outsourcing financial evaluation ⭐⭐⭐
- **First-year cost of outsourcing** = outsourcing price + one-off **transition cost** + annual **contract management** (governance) cost − any productivity savings in year 1.
- **Annual saving (steady state)** = current in-house cost − (outsourcing price + contract management) (+ productivity gain).
- **Payback period** = one-off investment (transition cost) ÷ annual saving.
- Also check **NPV** over the contract term, sensitivity, and qualitative factors (control, quality, risk, strategic fit). Approve if savings are material, payback is short, and risks are manageable through the contract.

## 13. Root-cause analysis for outsourcing failures: the Fishbone ⭐⭐
The **Ishikawa (fishbone, cause-and-effect) diagram** puts the problem (e.g. "temperature excursions & spoilage") at the head and groups causes on the bones — the **6Ms**: **Man** (people/training), **Machine** (reefer units, data loggers), **Method** (SOPs, pre-cooling, loading), **Material** (packaging, gel packs), **Measurement** (no real-time monitoring, uncalibrated sensors), **Mother nature/Environment** (heat, traffic delays). Add **Management/Contract** (no SLA on temperature, lowest-price carrier). Use with **5 Whys** and Pareto analysis; then corrective (fix now) and preventive (stop recurrence) actions — **CAPA**.

## Self-test
**Q1. Differentiate outsourcing and offshoring with an example.**
Outsourcing is about **who** does the work (a third party); offshoring is about **where** (another country). An Indian software firm hiring another Indian IT company is domestic outsourcing; opening its own development centre in Vietnam is captive offshoring; hiring a Vietnamese vendor would be offshore outsourcing.

**Q2. What is a weighted scoring model and what is its main weakness?**
Criteria are weighted to total 100, suppliers are rated on each, and Σ weight × rating gives a comparable score. Weakness: weights and ratings are subjective, so the result can flip with small weight changes; always run a sensitivity analysis and back the ratings with data.

**Q3. List five hidden costs of outsourcing.**
Supplier search and audits, travel, ERP/IT integration, quality inspection and rework, contract management/governance (also transition, coordination, buffer stock, exit costs).

**Q4. What is the difference between liquidated damages and a penalty?**
LDs are a genuine pre-estimate of the loss from a breach, agreed in advance, payable without proving actual loss; a penalty is an amount designed to punish/deter, which courts may not enforce in full (India s.74 allows only reasonable compensation up to the stated sum).

**Q5. Define BATNA and explain why it matters.**
Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement — your fallback if talks fail. It sets your walk-away point and your bargaining power; improving it (getting another quote) strengthens your position.

**Q6. When does force majeure apply?**
When performance is prevented by an event beyond the party's reasonable control that was not foreseeable or avoidable, which is listed in (or fits) the clause, the party gave timely notice, and it tried to mitigate. Effect: performance suspended, no damages; prolonged FM may allow termination.

**Q7. Explain 3PL vs 4PL.**
A 3PL performs logistics operations (warehousing, transport) under contract, often with its own assets; a 4PL is an integrator that designs and manages the whole supply chain and coordinates several 3PLs on the client's behalf, usually without owning assets.

**Q8. What makes a good SLA?**
Clear KPI definitions, realistic targets, an agreed measurement method and data source, reporting frequency, penalties and incentives, exclusions, and a governance/escalation process.
