## 1. Marine insurance — basics ⭐⭐⭐
**Marine insurance** is a contract by which the insurer undertakes to **indemnify** the assured against **marine losses** — losses incident to a marine adventure (Indian **Marine Insurance Act, 1963**, based on the UK MIA 1906). It covers the **hull** (ship), **cargo** and **freight** at risk, and liabilities.

### The three interests in a marine adventure ⭐⭐⭐
| Interest | Who owns it | Insurance |
|---|---|---|
| **Ship (hull & machinery)** | Shipowner | **Hull & Machinery (H&M)** policy |
| **Cargo** | Cargo owner(s) — shipper/consignee depending on Incoterm | **Marine cargo** policy (ICC A/B/C) |
| **Freight (at risk)** | Usually the carrier/shipowner (freight payable on delivery is lost if the cargo is lost) | Freight insurance / included in H&M |
Plus **liabilities** to third parties (collision, pollution, crew injury, cargo claims) — covered by **P&I (Protection & Indemnity) clubs**, mutual associations of shipowners.

### Principles of marine insurance ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Utmost good faith (uberrimae fidei)** — disclose all material facts (cargo nature, packing, route).
2. **Insurable interest** — the assured must stand to lose financially; in cargo, it must exist **at the time of loss** (who bears risk depends on the Incoterm).
3. **Indemnity** — restore the assured to its pre-loss financial position, no profit (agreed value in valued policies, usually CIF + 10%).
4. **Subrogation** — after paying, the insurer takes over the assured's rights against the party at fault (carrier, stevedore).
5. **Contribution** — if insured with several insurers, they share the loss.
6. **Proximate cause** — the loss must be proximately caused by an insured peril.
7. **Warranties** — conditions that must be strictly complied with (e.g. seaworthiness, legality).
8. **Mitigation (sue and labour)** — the assured must take reasonable steps to minimise loss; costs are recoverable.

### Types of cargo policies 📗
**Voyage policy** (one voyage), **time policy** (a period, usual for hulls), **mixed policy**, **valued** (value agreed) vs **unvalued** policy, **open cover / floating policy** (all shipments over a period declared as made — used by regular exporters), **blanket policy**, **warehouse-to-warehouse** cover (transit clause: from leaving the origin warehouse until delivery at the final warehouse, max 60 days after discharge).

### Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC 2009) ⭐⭐⭐
| | ICC (A) | ICC (B) | ICC (C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basis | **All risks** subject to exclusions | Named perils (wider) | Named perils (**minimum**) |
| Fire/explosion, vessel stranded/sunk/capsized, collision, overturning of land conveyance, discharge at port of distress | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| **General average sacrifice**, **jettison** | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| Earthquake, volcano, lightning | ✔ | ✔ | ✘ |
| Washing overboard; entry of sea/lake/river water into vessel, container or storage | ✔ | ✔ | ✘ |
| Total loss of a package dropped during loading/unloading | ✔ | ✔ | ✘ |
| Theft, pilferage, non-delivery, malicious damage, rough handling, rain, etc. | ✔ | ✘ | ✘ |
| Used by Incoterms | **CIP** minimum (2020) | — | **CIF** minimum |
**Exclusions in all**: wilful misconduct, ordinary leakage/wear and tear, **insufficient packing**, **inherent vice**, **delay**, insolvency of the owners/operators of the vessel, nuclear; and war and strikes unless the **Institute War Clauses** and **Institute Strikes Clauses** are added. Specialised: **ICC (Air)**, **Institute Frozen Food Clauses**, Commodity Trade Clauses. **GA contributions and salvage charges are recoverable under all three ICCs.**

## 2. Types of marine losses ⭐⭐⭐
```
Marine losses
├── Total loss
│   ├── Actual total loss (goods destroyed, irretrievably lost, or ceased to be the thing insured)
│   └── Constructive total loss (CTL) — loss reasonably abandoned because saving/repair would cost more than its value
│       (assured gives "notice of abandonment")
└── Partial loss
    ├── Particular Average (PA) — accidental partial loss falling only on the interest that suffered it
    ├── General Average (GA) — intentional sacrifice/expenditure for the common safety, shared by all interests
    └── Particular charges / sue & labour, salvage charges
```

### General Average ⭐⭐⭐
**General Average** is a principle of maritime law (dating to the **Rhodian Sea Law**, c. 800 BC — "what is thrown overboard for the benefit of all shall be made good by the contribution of all") under which a **loss arising from an extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure, intentionally and reasonably made or incurred, to preserve the ship and cargo from a common peril**, is **shared rateably** by all parties whose property was saved — ship, cargo and freight — in proportion to their **saved (contributory) values**.

**Conditions for a valid GA act** (York-Antwerp Rule A & MIA s.66) ⭐⭐⭐:
1. A **real and imminent peril** threatening the **whole adventure** (not just one interest).
2. The sacrifice or expenditure is **extraordinary** (beyond the ship's ordinary duties/costs).
3. It is **voluntary/intentional** (ordered, usually by the master) — not accidental.
4. It is **reasonable** (prudent in the circumstances).
5. It is made for the **common safety** of ship, cargo and freight.
6. **Something is saved** (success: the property must be preserved, at least in part).
7. The loss is the **direct consequence** of the GA act.

**Examples of GA sacrifices**: jettison of cargo; flooding holds/containers to fight fire (water damage to cargo); cutting away masts/rigging; deliberately beaching/stranding the ship; damage to engines when forcing a stranded ship off.
**Examples of GA expenditure**: salvage services for common safety; towage after breakdown; port-of-refuge costs (entering, discharging, storing, reloading cargo); emergency/temporary repairs needed to complete the voyage safely; hiring security escorts against an imminent pirate attack; lightering (transferring cargo to another vessel) to refloat; fuel and crew wages during detention at a port of refuge; firefighting expenses.

### Particular Average ⭐⭐⭐
An **accidental partial loss** caused by an insured peril that falls **only on the owner of the damaged property** — there is no sacrifice for the common good and no sharing. Recovered from that owner's **own insurer** (cargo policy or H&M), subject to the policy terms and excess. Example: heavy weather damages some containers of TVs; a fire damages part of the cargo before any GA act; engine failure itself (the breakdown, as distinct from towage to save the voyage).

### General Average vs Particular Average ⭐⭐⭐
| Basis | General Average | Particular Average |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Intentional/voluntary** sacrifice or expenditure | **Accidental** loss from a peril |
| Purpose | **Common safety** of the whole adventure | No common purpose; just a mishap |
| Who bears | **All interests** (ship, cargo, freight) contribute rateably | Only the **owner of the damaged property** |
| Basis of sharing | Contributory (saved) values | None (own loss) |
| Recovery | GA adjustment; contributions recoverable from cargo/H&M insurers | Owner's own insurance claim |
| Documents | GA bond, GA guarantee/deposit, adjuster's statement | Survey report, claim |
| Governed by | York-Antwerp Rules (via B/L & charter party) | Policy terms, MIA |
| Example | Jettison of wheat to stabilise ship in cyclone | Wheat damaged by seawater entering a cracked hold |

## 3. The York-Antwerp Rules (YAR) ⭐⭐
International rules for adjusting GA, first agreed **1864 (Glasgow)**, named after York (1864) and Antwerp (1877); revised **1890, 1924, 1950, 1974, 1994, 2004, 2016** (current: **YAR 2016**, drafted by the Comité Maritime International). They are **not law**: they apply because the **bill of lading, charter party and insurance policies incorporate them** ("General Average to be adjusted according to the York-Antwerp Rules 2016"). Structure: a **Rule of Interpretation**, a **Rule Paramount** (only reasonably made sacrifices/expenditures allowed), **lettered Rules A–G** (general principles: Rule A definition; Rule C only direct losses; Rule D GA rights not affected by fault, though fault may give claims later; Rule E burden of proof on the claimant; Rule G adjustment at values at the time and place where the adventure ends) and **numbered Rules I–XXIII** (specific items: jettison, fire extinguishing, port of refuge, temporary repairs, salvage, commission, interest).

## 4. The General Average calculation method ⭐⭐⭐
1. **Identify GA items** — sacrifices (value of cargo/ship property sacrificed) and expenditures (salvage, towage, cranes, security, temporary repairs). Exclude particular-average items and ordinary costs.
2. **Total GA loss** = Σ GA sacrifices + Σ GA expenditures.
3. **Total contributory value (CV)** = ship value + cargo values + freight at risk (each at its **arrived/saved value**, i.e. as at the end of the voyage, **plus amounts made good** for sacrificed property). In course problems the given values are taken as the contributory values.
4. **GA contribution rate (%)** = Total GA loss ÷ Total contributory value × 100.
5. **Each party's contribution** = its contributory value × GA rate.
6. **Settlement (net position)** = amount **made good** to the party (its sacrifice/expenditure allowed in GA) − its **contribution**. Positive → receives; negative → pays. The sum of net positions is always **zero** — a useful check.

> **Example logic**: if the master jettisons $250,000 of wheat, the wheat owner is *made good* $250,000 but also pays its own share of the GA (as a saved-value participant), so its net receipt is $250,000 − its contribution.

### The General Average adjuster ⭐⭐⭐
An independent specialist (average adjuster) appointed by the shipowner to:
1. Investigate the casualty and decide which losses and expenses qualify as GA under the YAR and the contract.
2. **Collect GA security** from cargo interests **before cargo is released**: a **GA bond** (Lloyd's Average Bond, signed by the consignee — undertaking to pay its share) plus a **GA guarantee** from the cargo insurer, or a **cash deposit**; plus commercial invoices to value the cargo.
3. Obtain surveys and values (ship's sound value, cargo's arrived values, freight at risk).
4. Compute **contributory values**, **GA rate**, each party's **contribution**, the amounts **made good**, interest and commission.
5. Issue the **GA adjustment (statement)** and settle balances among parties/insurers.
GA adjustments can take months or years; until security is posted, the shipowner has a **lien on the cargo**.

### Insurance and GA settlement ⭐⭐⭐
- **Cargo owner with marine cargo insurance**: the insurer issues the **GA guarantee** so the cargo is released quickly; it pays the **GA contribution** (and salvage charges) under all ICCs (A/B/C), up to the insured value, and pays the owner's **sacrifice** (claimed from the insurer, which then recovers via the GA adjustment — subrogation). If the cargo is **under-insured**, the insurer pays only proportionately (average clause).
- **Shipowner with H&M insurance**: the H&M insurer covers the ship's GA sacrifices and the ship's proportion of GA contribution, plus particular-average damage to the hull/machinery (repairs), subject to deductibles. P&I covers liabilities (e.g. cargo claims if the ship was unseaworthy).
- **Uninsured cargo owner**: must post a **cash deposit** (often 10–50% of cargo value, or more) or a bank guarantee before taking delivery; pays its contribution itself; receives nothing for its own particular-average losses; faces delays, storage and demurrage, and working capital locked up — a major reason never to ship uninsured.
- **Fault**: GA is shared even if the casualty was caused by the ship's fault (YAR Rule D); but if the carrier was **actionably at fault** (e.g. unseaworthy ship due to lack of due diligence), cargo can **refuse to contribute** or recover its contribution (Hague-Visby Art. III/IV; the *New Jason* clause in US trades).

### Why GA clauses are in bills of lading and policies ⭐⭐⭐
The B/L is the **contract of carriage**; the GA clause makes all cargo interests agree in advance that GA will be **adjusted under the York-Antwerp Rules**, at a named place, and gives the carrier a **lien** on cargo for GA security. This ensures: equitable sharing of losses incurred for everyone's benefit; the master can act decisively in an emergency without negotiating with hundreds of cargo owners; a uniform international basis across different laws; quick release of cargo against security; insurers cover GA contributions so traders are protected; and legal certainty in multimodal, multi-party container shipping (a mega-ship may carry 20,000 TEU for thousands of cargo owners — e.g. *Maersk Honam* fire 2018, *Ever Given* grounding in Suez 2021, *MSC Flaminia* 2012 and *X-Press Pearl* 2021, all declared GA).

## 5. Salvage ⭐⭐
**Salvage** is a voluntary service by a third party (salvor) that saves maritime property in danger; the reward is payable only on success ("**no cure, no pay**") under **Lloyd's Open Form (LOF)** and the **International Convention on Salvage 1989**; the reward is based on the saved value, skill, risk and danger. Salvage paid for the **common safety** is allowed in GA (YAR Rule VI). **SCOPIC** clause compensates salvors for environmental protection efforts even if property isn't saved.

## 6. Contracts of carriage and liability regimes ⭐⭐
| Regime | Year | Scope / key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Hague Rules** | 1924 | Carrier's minimum duties (seaworthiness by due diligence, care of cargo) and 17 defences (incl. **nautical fault**, fire); limit £100/package |
| **Hague-Visby Rules** | 1968/1979 | Limit **666.67 SDR per package or 2 SDR per kg**, whichever is higher; container clause — India's **Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1925** follows Hague(-Visby) |
| **Hamburg Rules** | 1978 | Removed nautical-fault defence; 835 SDR/package or 2.5 SDR/kg |
| **Rotterdam Rules** | 2008 | Door-to-door (maritime plus), e-documents; not yet in force |
| **Montreal Convention (air)** | 1999 | 22 SDR/kg (26 SDR from Dec 2024) |
| **CMR (road, Europe)** | 1956 | 8.33 SDR/kg |
Documents: **bill of lading** (receipt, evidence of contract, **document of title**), sea waybill (not a title document), charter parties (voyage, time, bareboat), air waybill (not negotiable), multimodal transport document (India: **Multimodal Transportation of Goods Act 1993**).

### Contract terms that link to insurance
- **Incoterms** decide who must insure (seller under CIF/CIP) and who holds insurable interest at the time of loss.
- **Insured value** = CIF + 10% normally.
- **Claims procedure**: notify carrier in writing (Hague-Visby: within **3 days** for non-apparent damage; Montreal: **14 days** for damage, 21 days for delay), joint survey, keep packing, file claim with documents (policy, invoice, packing list, B/L/AWB, survey report, letter of subrogation), recover from carrier within the time bar (**1 year** sea, 2 years air).
- **Deductible/excess**: the first part of each claim borne by the assured (e.g. 3% of value) — reduces premium.

## Self-test
**Q1. Define General Average and state its essential conditions.**
GA is an extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure intentionally and reasonably made or incurred for the common safety, to preserve property in a common maritime adventure from peril; the loss is shared by all saved interests in proportion to their values. Conditions: real, imminent common peril; extraordinary; intentional; reasonable; for common safety; property saved; loss is a direct consequence.

**Q2. Differentiate GA and PA.**
GA is intentional and for common safety, shared by ship, cargo and freight in proportion to contributory values; PA is an accidental partial loss borne only by the owner of the damaged property and claimed from its own insurer.

**Q3. What is the formula for the GA contribution rate?**
GA rate = total GA loss ÷ total contributory value (ship + cargo + freight at risk) × 100; each party pays its CV × rate.

**Q4. What do the York-Antwerp Rules do?**
They are the international rules for adjusting GA (latest YAR 2016), applied through incorporation in bills of lading, charter parties and insurance policies.

**Q5. Who is a GA adjuster and what security does cargo provide?**
An independent specialist who determines what is GA, collects security, values the interests and prepares the adjustment. Cargo provides a GA bond plus an insurer's GA guarantee or a cash deposit before release.

**Q6. Name the three Institute Cargo Clauses and which Incoterms use them.**
ICC (A) all risks — minimum for CIP; ICC (B) wider named perils; ICC (C) minimum named perils — minimum for CIF.

**Q7. What happens to an uninsured cargo owner in a GA?**
It must pay its contribution and post cash security before release, absorbs its own losses, suffers delays and locked-up cash, and recovers its own sacrifice only through the GA adjustment, net of its contribution.
