## Case 11.1 — Vibrant Video ⭐⭐⭐
Vibrant Video (V²) makes home theatre projection systems. It assembles Ultra HD projectors in **Portland, Oregon** and sells them online and through specialty electronics stores. The most popular model retails for **$4,995**, and V² bundles the projector with high-fidelity audio components bought from external suppliers. Because of delivery problems with Pacific Rim suppliers and carriers, V² will buy from suppliers closer to Portland: **speakers** from an electronics company in **Tijuana, Mexico**, and **receivers** from an audio lab in **Manchester, New Hampshire**. The speaker supplier offers two shipping choices under **FOB Destination, Freight Collect and Allowed**. The receiver supplier sells only **FOB Origin, Freight Collect**. The transportation director must evaluate the delivery options and decide.

| | Speakers | Receiver |
|---|---|---|
| Manufactured in | Tijuana, Mexico | Manchester, New Hampshire |
| V² purchase price | **$175 per set** | **$225 per unit** |
| Weight | 28 pounds | 5 pounds |
| Dimensions | 30" L × 18" W × 18" H | 18" L × 8" W × 4" H |
| Characteristics | Sturdy, bulky, not easily damaged | Compact, vibration sensitive, theft risk |
| Freight terms | FOB Destination, Freight Collect and Allowed | FOB Origin, Freight Collect |
| **Option 1** | Weekly **LTL** delivery, **200 units**, **$2,485** per delivery | Weekly **ground** delivery, **200 units**, **$2,169** per delivery |
| **Option 2** | Twice-per-month **TL** delivery, **400 units**, **$2,946** per delivery | Twice-per-week **airfreight** delivery, **100 units**, **$2,411** per delivery |

**Q1. What responsibilities, control and costs does V² bear under each of the FOB terms offered?**
Using Table 11.4:

| | Speakers — **FOB Destination, Freight Collect & Allowed** | Receivers — **FOB Origin, Freight Collect** |
|---|---|---|
| Owns goods in transit | **Supplier (seller)** — title and risk pass at V²'s dock in Portland | **V² (buyer)** — title and risk pass at the Manchester shipping point |
| Files freight claims (loss/damage) | Supplier | **V²** |
| Selects and pays the carrier | **V²** (freight collect) — V² **controls** mode, carrier and schedule | **V²** — full control |
| Ultimately bears freight cost | **Supplier** — V² pays the carrier, then **deducts the freight from its payment** to the supplier ("allowed") | **V²** |
| Best when | Buyer has greater influence with carriers | Buyer has greater influence with carriers |
**In short:** for speakers V² has **control without risk or (ultimate) cost** — it chooses and pays the carrier, the supplier owns the goods and handles claims, and the freight is recovered by deduction. For receivers V² has **full control, full risk and full cost** — it owns the goods from Manchester, files any claims (important for a theft-prone, vibration-sensitive product) and pays all freight. V² should insure the receivers in transit.

**Q2. What is the delivery cost and landed cost per unit for each speaker delivery option?**
Delivery cost per unit = cost per delivery ÷ units per delivery; landed cost per unit = purchase price + delivery cost per unit.

| Speakers | Option 1 — weekly LTL | Option 2 — twice-monthly TL |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per delivery | $2,485 | $2,946 |
| Units per delivery | 200 | 400 |
| **Delivery cost per unit** | 2,485 ÷ 200 = **$12.43** ($12.425) | 2,946 ÷ 400 = **$7.37** ($7.365) |
| **Landed cost per unit** | 175 + 12.425 = **$187.43** | 175 + 7.365 = **$182.37** |
| Deliveries per year | 52 | 24 |
| Annual freight / units | 52 × 2,485 = $129,220 for 10,400 units | 24 × 2,946 = $70,704 for 9,600 units |
Option 2 saves **$5.06 per unit** (≈ $52,600 a year on 10,400 units). Because the freight is **allowed** (deducted from the supplier's invoice), V²'s net outlay per speaker is the $175 price either way in the short run; but the landed cost is still the right basis for the decision — freight is a real supply chain cost the supplier will build into future prices, and V² pays it up front.

**Q3. Which delivery option do you recommend for the speakers?**
**Option 2 — twice-per-month truckload.**
- Lowest delivery and landed cost: $7.37 vs $12.43 per unit (−41%).
- Speakers are **sturdy, bulky and not easily damaged**, so the slower, less frequent TL service carries little damage risk; TL also avoids LTL terminal handling (less damage, faster, more reliable transit from Tijuana).
- Bulky product → LTL rates rise with cube; a full truck uses the space efficiently.
- Trade-off: larger order size raises average cycle inventory from 100 to 200 sets (200 ÷ 2 vs 400 ÷ 2). Extra 100 sets × $175 = $17,500 of inventory; at a **25% carrying-cost rate (assumption)** ≈ $4,375 a year — far less than the ≈ $52,000 freight saving. Check warehouse space for bulky cartons (each 30 × 18 × 18 in = 5.6 ft³; 400 sets ≈ 2,250 ft³).
- Cross-border Mexico → US: TL gives one customs crossing per load and simpler security (C-TPAT).

**Q4. What is the delivery cost and landed cost per unit for each receiver delivery option?**
| Receivers | Option 1 — weekly ground | Option 2 — twice-weekly airfreight |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per delivery | $2,169 | $2,411 |
| Units per delivery | 200 | 100 |
| **Delivery cost per unit** | 2,169 ÷ 200 = **$10.85** ($10.845) | 2,411 ÷ 100 = **$24.11** |
| **Landed cost per unit** | 225 + 10.845 = **$235.85** | 225 + 24.11 = **$249.11** |
| Deliveries per year | 52 | 104 |
| Annual freight (10,400 units) | 52 × 2,169 = **$112,788** | 104 × 2,411 = **$250,744** |
Option 1 saves **$13.27 per unit**, about **$137,956 a year**.

**Q5. Which delivery option do you recommend for the receivers?**
**Option 1 — weekly ground delivery**, with safeguards.
- Landed cost $235.85 vs $249.11: airfreight more than doubles the freight bill for the same annual volume.
- Under FOB Origin, Freight Collect, V² bears the **risk** — so manage the receiver's weaknesses (vibration sensitivity, theft risk) through **protective packaging** (foam, shock indicators), a **carrier with a good loss-and-damage record**, sealed/secure trailers, tracking, and **cargo insurance**.
- Inventory: weekly lots of 200 give average stock of 100 units ($22,500) vs 50 ($11,250) with air; extra carrying cost at 25% ≈ $2,800 a year — tiny vs the $138,000 freight saving. Receivers are **compact**, so holding a week's stock needs little space.
- Choose air (Option 2) only if damage/theft by ground proves high (each lost receiver costs $225 plus lost projector sales), or for urgent replenishment — a hybrid with **expedited air as a backup** is sensible.

**Q6. What other supply chain issues and costs must V² take into consideration when making these transportation decisions?**
- **Inventory carrying cost and storage space** (order size vs frequency) and the **total cost** view (freight + inventory + handling + loss/damage).
- **Transit time and reliability** (variability) and their effect on **safety stock** and assembly schedules; matching speaker and receiver arrivals to projector assembly (bundles need all three).
- **Loss, damage and theft** risk and claims administration (V² files claims for receivers); **insurance**.
- **Supplier relationships and freight terms** — the "allowed" arrangement could change; negotiate FOB terms and freight allowances.
- **Cross-border issues** for Tijuana: customs brokerage, USMCA origin rules, duties, C-TPAT security, border delays, currency (MXN).
- **Carrier selection and performance** — use a scorecard (on-time, damage, billing accuracy) and contract rates; fuel surcharges.
- **Demand variability and forecasting**; customer service levels online and in stores; stock-out costs (a missing $175 speaker blocks a $4,995 sale).
- **Packaging, handling and receiving** labour at Portland (TL vs LTL unloading).
- **Sustainability** — TL and ground produce far less CO₂ than LTL multi-stop routes and air.
- **Administrative costs** — number of deliveries to schedule, receive, invoice and pay (52 + 24 vs 52 + 104 transactions).

## Case 11.2 — Bob's Custom BBQs ⭐⭐⭐
Bob's Custom BBQs, a manufacturer of outdoor grills, uses three primary carriers to move grills from its Texas factory to home improvement retailers in the United States and Canada. The owner, **Bob Flambeau**, wants to evaluate the three carriers over a three-month period.

| Performance criteria | Allied Transport | Bestway Freight | Certaint Carriers |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-time delivery | 99.5% | 98.7% | 98.2% |
| Loads with damage claims | 0.9% | 1.6% | 0.4% |
| Customer satisfaction ratings | 4.6 | 4.2 | 3.9 |
| Billing accuracy | 99.3% | 99.6% | 98.2% |
| Loads rejected | 1.3% | 2.1% | 0.9% |

The transportation manager's scorecard:
| Performance criteria | Weight factor | Performance evaluation | Potential score |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-time delivery | 30 | >98.5% = 5; 96.01–98.5% = 4; 93–96% = 2; <93% = 0 | 150 |
| Loads with damage claims | 30 | <0.5% = 5; 0.5–1% = 4; 1.01–2% = 2; >2% = 0 | 150 |
| Customer satisfaction ratings | 20 | >4.5 = 5; 4.01–4.5 = 4; 3–4 = 2; <3 = 0 | 100 |
| Billing accuracy | 10 | >99% = 5; 97.01–99% = 4; 95–97% = 2; <95% = 0 | 50 |
| Loads rejected | 10 | <1% = 5; 1%–2% = 4; 2.01–3% = 2; >3% = 0 | 50 |
| **Total** | 100 | | **500** |

**Q7. Calculate the performance score for each of the three carriers.**
Category score = weight × points.

| Criteria (weight) | Allied Transport | Bestway Freight | Certaint Carriers |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-time delivery (30) | 99.5% > 98.5% → 5 → **150** | 98.7% > 98.5% → 5 → **150** | 98.2% (96.01–98.5) → 4 → **120** |
| Damage claims (30) | 0.9% (0.5–1) → 4 → **120** | 1.6% (1.01–2) → 2 → **60** | 0.4% (< 0.5) → 5 → **150** |
| Customer satisfaction (20) | 4.6 > 4.5 → 5 → **100** | 4.2 (4.01–4.5) → 4 → **80** | 3.9 (3–4) → 2 → **40** |
| Billing accuracy (10) | 99.3% > 99 → 5 → **50** | 99.6% > 99 → 5 → **50** | 98.2% (97.01–99) → 4 → **40** |
| Loads rejected (10) | 1.3% (1–2) → 4 → **40** | 2.1% (2.01–3) → 2 → **20** | 0.9% (< 1) → 5 → **50** |
| **Total (out of 500)** | **460 (92%)** | **360 (72%)** | **400 (80%)** |
Ranking: **Allied 460 > Certaint 400 > Bestway 360.**

**Q8. Which carrier would you recommend that Bob consider for elimination? Why?**
**Bestway Freight (360/500).** It has the lowest total score and is worst on the two most damaging operational metrics after on-time delivery: **damage claims 1.6%** (4× Certaint's rate; weight 30) and **loads rejected 2.1%** (the highest; it refuses Bob's loads, forcing re-tendering at higher cost). Its customer satisfaction (4.2) is also middling. Its strengths (on-time 98.7%, billing 99.6%) do not offset damage, which hurts retailers and Bob's brand. Before eliminating, Bob should share the scorecard and check volumes/lanes — but on this evidence Bestway's freight should move to Allied and Certaint. (Allied is the best all-round carrier; Certaint is the safest for damage.)

**Q9. If Bob decides to keep all three carriers, what should each of them work to improve?**
- **Allied Transport (460)** — **damage claims** (0.9% → below 0.5% for full points: better load securement, handling training, equipment) and **loads rejected** (1.3% → below 1%: commit capacity, honour tenders via a contract/dedicated capacity).
- **Bestway Freight (360)** — **damage claims** (1.6% is serious: root-cause analysis, packaging/blocking & bracing, driver training, equipment), **loads rejected** (2.1%: capacity planning and tender acceptance), and **customer satisfaction** (4.2 → above 4.5: communication, appointment compliance at retailers).
- **Certaint Carriers (400)** — **customer satisfaction** (3.9 is the lowest: driver courtesy, delivery appointments, proactive communication, proof of delivery), **on-time delivery** (98.2% → above 98.5%) and **billing accuracy** (98.2% → above 99%: audit invoices, EDI 210 electronic billing).
Bob should hold quarterly reviews, set targets and reward improvement with more freight volume.

## Pre-read worked example — Table 11.8 scorecard ⭐⭐
**Q10. Using Table 11.8, show how the carrier's total score of 405 is obtained and interpret it.**
On-time delivery: 96.7% falls in 96.01–98% → 4 points × weight 35 = **140**. Loss & damage: 0.6% in 0.5–1% → 4 × 30 = **120**. Billing accuracy: 98.1% in 97–99% → 3 × 15 = **45**. Equipment condition: acceptable (safe & clean) → 5 × 5 = **25**. Customer service: outstanding survey feedback → superior 5 × 15 = **75**. **Total = 140 + 120 + 45 + 25 + 75 = 405** of a possible 500 (81%). The carrier is good but should improve **on-time delivery** (to > 98%) and **billing accuracy** (to > 99%), where it loses 35 and 30 points respectively.
